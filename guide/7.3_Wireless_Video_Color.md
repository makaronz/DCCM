# 7.3 Wireless Video and Color Accuracy

## Theory: Wireless Compression and Impact on Color

### Wireless Video Transmission

**Frequency Bands:**
- **5GHz**: Standard for high-quality video (less interference, shorter range ~500ft line of sight)
- **2.4GHz**: Longer range (~1000ft) but crowded (Wi-Fi, Bluetooth, microwaves), not recommended for critical monitoring
- **60GHz (V-band)**: Ultra-high bandwidth (uncompressed 4K60), very short range (~100ft), line of sight required

**Transmission Methods:**
- ** uncompressed**: Teradek Bolt 4K, SmallHD Focus Bolt (up to 4K30, latency <1ms, expensive, limited range)
- **Compressed (H.264/H.265)**: Most consumer/prosumer wireless (lower bitrate, longer range, introduces artifacts)
- **Proprietary**: Paralinx, Video Assist (optimized for low latency, but still compressed)

### Compression and Color Fidelity

**H.264 (AVC) Compression:**
- **8-bit Color**: Limited to 8-bit (4:2:0 typically), banding in gradients, color precision loss
- **4:2:0 Chroma Subsampling**: Color resolution quarter of luma (blurred color edges, color bleeding)
- **Macroblocking**: 8x8 or 16x16 pixel blocks visible in flat areas (sky, walls), compression artifacts
- **Bitrate**: 20-100 Mbps typical (wireless monitors), higher bitrate = fewer artifacts but more bandwidth required

**H.265 (HEVC) Compression:**
- **10-bit Support**: Better color precision (smoother gradients, less banding), still often limited to 8-bit in wireless
- **4:2:0 Chroma**: Same color subsampling as H.264, but more efficient compression (better quality at lower bitrate)
- **Compression Efficiency**: 50% better than H.264 (same quality at half bitrate), but more processing latency
- **Bitrate**: 10-50 Mbps typical (wireless monitors), lower bitrate than H.264 for similar quality

**JPEG XS (ISO/IEC 21122):**
- **Visually Lossless**: Mezzanine codec (light compression), artifacts imperceptible to human eye
- **Low Latency**: <1 frame delay (faster than H.264/H.265), suitable for live monitoring
- **High Quality**: 4:4:4 or 4:2:2 chroma, 10/12-bit color, minimal compression (2:1 to 6:1 ratio)
- **Adoption**: Newer wireless systems (Teradek Bolt 4K X), IP video infrastructure (ST 2110)

**Compression Impact on Color:**
- **Banding**: Visible steps in gradients (sky, skin tones) due to 8-bit color + compression
- **Color Bleeding**: Color fringing around edges (chroma subsampling + macroblocking)
- **Macroblocking**: Visible blocks in flat areas (low bitrate or complex motion)
- **Color Shifts**: Slight hue/saturation changes (lossy compression, chroma subsampling)

### Latency and Real-Time Monitoring

**Latency Sources:**
- **Encoding**: Camera → wireless transmitter (H.264/H.265 encoding takes 1-2 frames)
- **Transmission**: Wireless signal travel time (negligible at speed of light, but buffering adds latency)
- **Decoding**: Receiver → monitor (H.264/H.265 decoding takes 1-2 frames)
- **Total Latency**: 2-6 frames (80-240ms at 24fps) for compressed wireless, <1 frame (<40ms) for uncompressed

**Acceptable Latency:**
- **Focus Pulling**: <80ms (2 frames at 24fps) critical for pulling focus from wireless monitor
- **Client Monitoring**: 100-200ms acceptable (not pulling focus, just evaluating composition/performance)
- **Live Grading**: <80ms preferred (real-time feedback, though grading decisions can tolerate more latency)
- **Playback Review**: Latency irrelevant (not live, can tolerate higher compression)

**Latency Reduction Techniques:**
- **Uncompressed Wireless**: Teradek Bolt (low latency, but limited range and expensive)
- **Higher Bitrate**: Reduces encoding/decoding complexity (less compression = faster processing)
- **Wired Monitoring**: SDI cable (zero latency, ideal for critical decisions)
- **Local Monitoring**: Director/focus puller wired, client wireless

## Practice: Wireless Hardware Setup

### Wireless Video Transmitters

**Teradek Bolt 4K/6K:**
- **Specs**: Up to 4K60 10-bit 4:2:2, uncompressed, <1ms latency, 500ft range (5GHz)
- **Use Case**: High-end production, focus pulling, live grading, critical monitoring
- **Setup**: Connect camera SDI out to transmitter, mount on camera, pair receiver(s)
- **Cost**: $6000-8000 (transmitter + receiver), expensive but professional standard

**SmallHD Focus Bolt:**
- **Specs**: Up to 1080p60 8-bit 4:2:0, uncompressed, <1ms latency, 500ft range
- **Use Case**: Focus pulling, director monitoring, budget-conscious production
- **Setup**: Monitor with built-in Bolt receiver, no separate receiver needed
- **Cost**: $2000-3000 (monitor with Bolt), more affordable than Teradek

**Blackmagic Video Assist 7" 12G HDR:**
- **Specs**: Up to 4K60 10-bit 4:2:2, H.265 wireless (optional), 100ft range (Wi-Fi)
- **Use Case**: Consumer wireless, client monitoring, non-critical evaluation
- **Setup**: Monitor with wireless transmitter, connect to camera SDI/HDMI
- **Cost**: $1500 (monitor only), affordable but compressed wireless

**Teradek Sidekick:**
- **Specs**: Up to 1080p60 8-bit 4:2:0, H.264 compressed, 300ft range (5GHz)
- **Use Case**: Budget wireless, video village, reference monitoring
- **Setup**: Connect to camera HDMI/SDI, pair with iPhone/iPad (Teradek app)
- **Cost**: $600 (transmitter only), cheapest option for client monitoring

### Wireless Monitor Configuration

**Signal Quality Optimization:**
- **Line of Sight**: Clear path between transmitter and receiver (no walls, crew, equipment blocking)
- **Antenna Positioning**: Orient antennas vertically (polarity matching), mount high on stands
- **Frequency Selection**: Use 5GHz band (less interference than 2.4GHz), scan for clean channels
- **Bitrate Settings**: Maximize bitrate (uncompressed if available), reduce if signal drops (artifacts)

**Monitoring Setup:**
- **Primary Monitor**: DIT cart (wired SDI from camera, zero latency, accurate color)
- **Client Monitor**: Wireless (compressed, acceptable for composition/performance evaluation)
- **Focus Puller**: Wired (if possible) or uncompressed wireless (Teradek Bolt)
- **Director**: Wireless or wired (depending on distance and criticality)

**CDL/LUT over Wireless:**
- **Challenge**: Wireless monitors often don't support CDL/LUT overlay (hardware limitation)
- **Solution**: Bake LUT into camera output (if available), or accept flat look for wireless monitoring
- **Alternative**: Use software (LiveGrade) to apply LUT before wireless transmission (requires hardware)

### Wireless Color Accuracy

**Best Practices:**
- **Color Decisions on Wired**: Always use wired SDI for critical color evaluation (focus pulling, grading)
- **Wireless for Reference**: Accept that wireless is for composition/performance, not accurate color
- **Test Before Shoot**: Verify wireless quality (look for banding, blocking, color shifts)
- **Inform Client**: Explain wireless limitations (compressed, not color-accurate)

**Color Accuracy Tests:**
1. **Gradient Test**: Display smooth gradient (black to white) on monitor, look for banding on wireless
2. **Skin Tone Test**: Display skin tone patches, check for color shifts or banding
3. **Motion Test**: Pan camera, look for macroblocking or artifacts in motion
4. **Comparison**: Compare wireless feed to wired SDI (same monitor, different inputs)

**Wireless Quality Settings:**
- **High Quality**: Uncompressed or visually lossless (Teradek Bolt, JPEG XS), bitrate >1Gbps
- **Medium Quality**: H.265 50Mbps, 4:2:2 10-bit (minimal artifacts, acceptable for client monitoring)
- **Low Quality**: H.264 20Mbps, 4:2:0 8-bit (banding, blocking, only for reference)

## Failure Modes: Compression and Transmission

### Compression Artifacts

**Banding in Gradients:**
- **Symptom**: Visible steps in smooth gradients (sky, walls, skin highlights), 8-bit color + compression
- **Cause**: 8-bit wireless (H.264/H.265 4:2:0), low bitrate (<30Mbps), steep gradients
- **Fix**: Raise bitrate (50Mbps+ for H.265, 100Mbps+ for H.264), use 10-bit wireless (if available)
- **Prevention**: Avoid steep gradients (add subtle noise), grade for wireless monitoring (reduce contrast), use wired for color decisions

**Macroblocking:**
- **Symptom**: 8x8 or 16x16 pixel blocks visible in flat areas (sky, walls, out-of-focus backgrounds)
- **Cause**: Low bitrate (<20Mbps), complex motion (camera pans, fast action), I-frame interval too long
- **Fix**: Increase bitrate, reduce I-frame interval (more frequent keyframes), stabilize camera (less motion)
- **Prevention**: Test wireless with worst-case scenario (fast pan, complex scene), plan for higher bitrate or wired monitoring

**Color Bleeding:**
- **Symptom**: Color fringes around edges (red/blue halos around objects), especially in high-contrast areas
- **Cause**: 4:2:0 chroma subsampling (color resolution ¼ of luma), compression artifacts
- **Fix**: Use 4:2:2 wireless (if available), increase bitrate, reduce edge enhancement (camera settings)
- **Prevention**: Accept color bleeding as wireless limitation (not for color decisions), use wired for critical evaluation

**Color Shifts:**
- **Symptom**: Skin tones appear too red/magenta, greens shift to yellow/blue, overall hue shift
- **Cause**: Lossy compression (chroma quantization), color space conversion (Rec.709 → Rec.601), wrong color matrix
- **Fix**: Verify color space settings (Rec.709 on both transmitter and receiver), match color matrix (ITU-R BT.709)
- **Prevention**: Test color accuracy (display color bars on wireless, compare to wired), document known color shifts

### Signal Loss and Interference

**Signal Dropouts:**
- **Symptom**: Video freezes, glitches, or goes black intermittently (especially with movement)
- **Cause**: Obstacles (crew, equipment blocking line of sight), distance >500ft, interference (other 5GHz devices)
- **Fix**: Clear line of sight (raise antennas on stands), move closer (reduce distance), change frequency channel
- **Prevention**: Test wireless range before shoot (walk test with monitor), have backup wired SDI

**Interference from Other Devices:**
- **Symptom**: Signal degrades (artifacts increase) when other wireless devices are used (Wi-Fi, mic receivers)
- **Cause**: 5GHz band crowded (multiple wireless systems), channel overlap (adjacent frequencies)
- **Fix**: Scan for clean channels (wireless software tools), use frequency coordination (assign unique channels)
- **Prevention**: Turn off unnecessary Wi-Fi (hotspot on set), use licensed spectrum (if available), coordinate with sound department

**Multipath Interference:**
- **Symptom**: Signal flickers, artifacts appear when moving (reflected signals cancel direct signal)
- **Cause**: Signals reflecting off metal surfaces (trucks, equipment), creating ghosting
- **Fix**: Reposition antennas (avoid reflective surfaces), use directional antennas (focus signal)
- **Prevention**: Avoid placing transmitter/receiver near metal surfaces, test for multipath (move antennas)

### CDL Not Transmitting

**Missing CDL on Wireless Monitor:**
- **Symptom**: Graded image on DIT cart, but flat image on wireless client monitor
- **Cause**: Wireless monitor doesn't support CDL/LUT overlay (hardware limitation), or CDL not embedded in signal
- **Fix**: Bake LUT into camera output (if camera supports custom LUT upload), or accept flat image on wireless
- **Prevention**: Inform client that wireless is for reference, not color-accurate monitoring

**CDL Format Incompatibility:**
- **Symptom**: CDL values saved on set, but post can't read them (wrong format, metadata missing)
- **Cause**: CDL embedded in proxy files (ProRes) but not in raw files, or wrong CDL format (ASC-CDL vs proprietary)
- **Fix**: Export CDL as ALE/XML (separate from video files), use industry-standard formats (ASC-CDL)
- **Prevention**: Test CDL round-trip (on-set → post → back to on-set), document CDL workflow

**Timecode Mismatch:**
- **Symptom**: CDL applied to wrong shot in post (wrong timecode, clip mismatch)
- **Cause**: CDL saved with wrong timecode format (DF vs NDF), camera timecode not jammed correctly
- **Fix**: Verify timecode format (use NDF for 24fps, DF for 29.97), jam-sync all cameras to master clock
- **Prevention**: Test CDL export (open ALE in text editor, verify timecode matches camera reports)

### Wrong Color Space Over Wireless

**Rec.709 vs P3 Mismatch:**
- **Symptom**: Colors oversaturated on wireless (P3 content on Rec.709 monitor), or washed out (Rec.709 on P3)
- **Cause**: Camera outputting P3, but wireless monitor calibrated to Rec.709 (gamut mismatch)
- **Fix**: Set camera output to Rec.709 for wireless monitoring (separate output from RAW recording)
- **Prevention**: Document camera output settings (Rec.709 for monitoring, P3 for recording), test wireless before shoot

**SDR vs HDR Mismatch:**
- **Symptom**: Image appears too dark (HDR on SDR monitor), or washed out (SDR on HDR monitor)
- **Cause**: HDR content (PQ/HLG) displayed on SDR monitor (wrong EOTF), or SDR on HDR monitor
- **Fix**: Set monitor to correct mode (SDR for Rec.709, HDR for PQ/HLG), or convert HDR to SDR for wireless
- **Prevention**: Know delivery format (HDR vs SDR), test monitoring setup before shoot (display test pattern)

**Bit Depth Mismatch (8-bit vs 10-bit):**
- **Symptom**: Banding on wireless but not on wired (10-bit source displayed on 8-bit wireless)
- **Cause**: Wireless monitor limited to 8-bit (consumer/prosumer), can't display 10-bit gradients
- **Fix**: Accept 8-bit limitation (wireless for reference only), or upgrade to 10-bit wireless (Teradek Bolt 4K)
- **Prevention**: Test wireless with 10-bit gradient (look for banding), document known limitations

## Mini Glossary

- **Wireless Video**: Video transmission over radio frequencies (5GHz, 2.4GHz) without cables, enabling client monitoring and focus pulling

- **Compression**: Reducing video data size (H.264/H.265), introducing artifacts (banding, blocking) but enabling wireless transmission

- **Latency**: Delay between camera capture and monitor display (2-6 frames for compressed wireless, <1 frame for uncompressed)

- **Bitrate**: Data rate (Mbps) for compressed video, higher bitrate = fewer artifacts but more bandwidth required

- **CDL Transmission**: Sending color decisions (SOP/SAT) over wireless (limited support, often requires wired monitoring for accurate color)

- **Color Fidelity**: Accuracy of color reproduction over wireless (limited by 8-bit color, 4:2:0 chroma subsampling, compression)

- **Line of Sight**: Unobstructed path between wireless transmitter and receiver (critical for signal strength, range)

- **Macroblocking**: Compression artifact (8x8 or 16x16 pixel blocks) visible in flat areas due to low bitrate or high motion

- **Band STE**: Visible steps in smooth gradients due to 8-bit color + compression (especially in sky, skin highlights)

- **Interference**: Signal disruption from other wireless devices (Wi-Fi, mic receivers) or physical obstacles (crew, equipment)
