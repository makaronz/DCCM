# 2.2 Sony Cameras (VENICE 2, BURANO)

## Theory: Sony Color Science

### Venice Color Engine (VCE)

**VCE** [Venice Color Engine: Sony's proprietary color processing pipeline designed for cinematic image quality with accurate skin tones and wide dynamic range] introduced with VENICE (2017) and enhanced in VENICE 2 (2022).

**Core principles**:
1. **Full-frame optimization**: Color response tuned for 36×24mm sensor format
2. **Dual-base ISO processing**: Separate signal paths for 500/2500 (VENICE) or 800/3200 (VENICE 2)
3. **Highlight roll-off**: Emulates film-like highlight softening
4. **Skin tone accuracy**: Specifically optimized for multi-ethnic skin reproduction

**VCE 2.0** (VENICE 2, BURANO):
- Improved color matrix vs VCE 1.0
- Better low-light color accuracy (3200 ISO base)
- Enhanced highlight handling (less color crosstalk)
- S-Cinetone compatibility (built-in look)

### Sensor Architecture

**VENICE 2 sensor options**:

**8.6K sensor** (flagship, 2022):
- Dimensions: 36.2mm × 24.1mm (full-frame)
- Resolution: 8640 × 5760 (8.6K)
- Photosite size: 4.19µm × 4.19µm
- Base ISO: 800/3200 (dual-base)
- Dynamic range: 16+ stops (claimed)
- OLPF: Dual-layer (reducible aliasing)

**6K sensor** (legacy VENICE sensor):
- Dimensions: 36.2mm × 24.1mm (full-frame)
- Resolution: 6048 × 4032 (6K)
- Photosite size: 5.99µm × 5.99µm
- Base ISO: 500/2500 (dual-base)
- Dynamic range: 15+ stops

**BURANO sensor** (2024):
- Dimensions: 36.2mm × 24.1mm (full-frame)
- Resolution: 8640 × 5760 (8.6K)
- Photosite size: 4.19µm × 4.19µm
- Base ISO: 800/3200 (dual-base)
- Dynamic range: 16+ stops (claimed)
- Electronic ND (optional)

**Dual-base ISO** [Sony's dual-sensitivity sensor design with optimized circuitry for high and low ISO ranges]:
- **Low base** (800 or 500): Standard mode, optimal SNR
- **High base** (3200 or 2500): High sensitivity mode, cleaner shadows at high ISO
- Switching changes analog gain path, not digital gain
- Different noise floor but similar dynamic range

**Noise performance** (VENICE 2, 8.6K sensor):
- Read noise at ISO 800: ~3.5e-
- Read noise at ISO 3200: ~5.2e-
- Full well capacity: ~65,000e-
- SNR at 18% gray: 54dB (ISO 800), 51dB (ISO 3200)

### S-Log3 Encoding

**S-Log3** [Sony's logarithmic encoding introduced with F55/F5 in 2015, optimized for 14+ stop dynamic range and single-curve simplicity]:

$$
V = \begin{cases}
420 + \log_{10}(5 \cdot L) \cdot 261.5 & \text{for } L \geq 0 \\
\text{linear portion} & \text{for } L < 0
\end{cases}
$$

**Key characteristics**:
- Base 10 logarithm (no power-law preprocessing like LogC4)
- Single curve for all exposure scenarios
- Normalized scene linear $L=0.0$ maps to CV 95/1023
- 18% gray maps to CV 388/1023
- 100% reflectance maps to CV 596/1023
- Clip point at 10 stops above 18% gray = CV 939/1023

**Comparison to LogC4**:
- **LogC4**: 18% gray at CV 599 (brighter appearance)
- **S-Log3**: 18% gray at CV 388 (darker appearance)
- **Implication**: Same exposure appears darker on Sony vs ARRI
- **Grading**: Grade S-Log3 "up" to match LogC4 brightness

**CineEI mode** [Cine Exposure Index: Sony's ISO-independent exposure workflow where EI adjusts metadata but not gain]:
- Set EI based on desired exposure index (EI 200-2560)
- Camera records at base ISO (800/3200 for VENICE 2)
- EI change affects monitoring LUT, not recorded data
- Preserve highlight headroom regardless of EI setting

**EI workflow**:
- EI 800: Base, recommended for most situations
- EI 1600: Brighter monitoring, 1 stop less highlight headroom
- EI 3200: Low-light, 2 stops less highlight headroom
- EI 400: More highlight headroom, darker monitoring

### S-Gamut Gamuts

**S-Gamut3** [Sony's widest color gamut, larger than Rec.2020, designed for maximum color capture]:

**Primary chromaticities** (approximate):
- Red: (0.730, 0.265) - beyond Rec.2020
- Green: (0.140, 0.855) - beyond Rec.2020
- Blue: (0.090, -0.045) - beyond Rec.2020
- White: D65 (0.3127, 0.3290)

**Gamut coverage**:
- vs DCI-P3: ~125%
- vs Rec.2020: ~105%
- vs Pointer's gamut: ~100% of real surface colors

**S-Gamut3.Cine** [slightly smaller than S-Gamut3, designed for easier grading with P3-like behavior]:
- Saturated colors contained within P3 boundary
- Easier to map to deliverable gamuts
- Less aggressive saturation adjustments needed
- Recommended for most productions

**Gamut selection strategy**:
- **S-Gamut3**: Maximum color capture, VFX-heavy work, archival
- **S-Gamut3.Cine**: Easier grading, narrative, commercial work
- **S-Gamut3.SW** [software emulation of S-Gamut3 from Hypergamut capture]

### X-OCN Codec

**X-OCN** [eXtended Tonal Range Original Camera Negative: Sony's compressed RAW format offering 16-bit linear quality with reduced file size] introduced with VENICE (2017).

**Compression levels**:
- **X-OCN XT** [eXtended Tone - highest quality]: Visually lossless, 16-bit quality
- **X-OCN ST** [Standard Tone]: Balance of quality and file size
- **X-OCN LT** [Lite Tone]: Maximum compression for longer record times

**Technical details**:
- 16-bit linear data (not logarithmic)
- Bitrate: ~500 Mbps (XT), ~300 Mbps (ST), ~200 Mbps (LT) for 6K 24fps
- Compression: Wavelet-based, proprietary Sony algorithm
- Quality: Visually indistinguishable from uncompressed RAW at ST and XT

**Advantages over uncompressed RAW**:
- Smaller file sizes (3-5x reduction)
- Faster transfer times
- Lower storage costs
- Maintains RAW flexibility (WB, ISO adjustment in post)

**Comparison to REDCODE RAW**:
- X-OCN: Wavelet compression, 16-bit, Sony-specific
- REDCODE: Wavelet compression, 16-bit, RED-specific
- Similar quality at similar compression ratios

## Practice: S-Log3 Exposure, CineEI Mode, S-Cinetone

### S-Log3 Exposure Strategy

**Middle gray placement**:
- 18% gray card at CV 388/1023 (S-Log3)
- Waveform monitor: 38-41% IRE with S-Log3 LUT
- False color: Skin tones at 45-55% IRE with S-Log3 LUT

**Exposure metering**:
- Incident meter: Set ISO to EI value, meter normally
- Reflected meter: 18% gray at f/2.8 (example), verify on waveform
- Spot meter: Key face at zone 6 (CV 450-500 for S-Log3)

**ETTR (Expose To The Right)**:
- Maximize signal without clipping
- Use histogram to verify no right-edge pile-up
- Reserve 2-3 stops above key face for highlights
- Underexposure = noisy shadows (log doesn't help underexposure)

**Practical exposure values** (CineEI mode):
- Key face: CV 450-500 (S-Log3, 10-bit)
- 18% gray: CV 388 (S-Log3, 10-bit)
- 90% reflectance: CV 596 (S-Log3, 10-bit)
- Clip point: CV 939 (S-Log3, 10-bit)

### CineEI Mode Workflow

**CineEI vs Custom mode**:
- **CineEI**: ISO-independent, recommended for cinema production
- **Custom**: Traditional ISO mode with gain applied, not recommended for S-Log3

**CineEI workflow**:
1. **Select base ISO**: 800 (daylight/high-key) or 3200 (tungsten/low-light)
2. **Set EI**: Based on desired monitoring brightness (EI 200-2560)
3. **Monitor with LUT**: Apply S-Log3 to Rec.709 LUT for monitoring
4. **Expose for highlights**: Protect from clipping regardless of EI setting
5. **Verify exposure**: Waveform monitor, false color, skin tones

**EI recommendations**:
- **EI 800**: Base, optimal SNR, most common
- **EI 1600**: Brighter monitoring for dim viewing environments
- **EI 3200**: Low-light situations, 2 stops less highlight headroom
- **EI 400-640**: More highlight headroom for high-contrast scenes

**Common mistake**: Treating EI as traditional ISO. EI changes monitoring, not recorded signal. Camera always records at base ISO (800 or 3200) in CineEI mode.

### S-Cinetone Workflow

**S-Cinetone** [Sony's built-in look designed for pleasing out-of-camera images with skin tone optimization and film-like contrast] introduced with FX9 (2020) and added to VENICE 2/BURANO.

**Characteristics**:
- Slight contrast boost (1.1 gamma approx)
- Mild saturation increase (1.2x approx)
- Skin tone warmth (subtle orange-red shift)
- Highlight roll-off emulation (film-like)

**Usage scenarios**:
- **Broadcast**: Deliverable footage without grading
- **Documentary**: Fast turnaround, minimal color work
- **Dailies**: Client preview with near-final look
- **Not recommended**: VFX work (baked-in look complicates keying)

**S-Cinetone vs S-Log3 grading**:
- **S-Cinetone**: Already graded, limited flexibility
- **S-Log3**: Scene-referred, maximum flexibility
- **Hybrid**: Shoot S-Log3, monitor with S-Cinetone LUT

**S-Cinetone exposure**:
- Different target than S-Log3
- 18% gray at CV 400-450 (higher than S-Log3's CV 388)
- Skin tones at CV 550-600
- Less highlight headroom than S-Log3

## Failure Modes

### S-Log3/S-Gamut3 Confusion

**Symptom**:
- Footage appears oversaturated or undersaturated
- Skin tones look unnatural (too red or too green)
- Color grading produces unexpected shifts

**Root cause**:
- S-Log3 recorded with S-Gamut3 but decoded as S-Gamut3.Cine
- Or vice versa (S-Gamut3.Cine recorded, decoded as S-Gamut3)
- Wrong IDT applied in post

**Detection**:
- Skin tones look wrong (too magenta or too green)
- Color vectorscope shows saturation mismatch
- Check metadata: "S-Gamut3" vs "S-Gamut3.Cine"

**Solution**:
1. Apply correct IDT (S-Gamut3 or S-Gamut3.Cine)
2. Verify with skin tones
3. Re-render dailies with correct transform
4. Document correct settings for future reference

**Prevention**:
- Camera report should specify S-Gamut variant
- Embed gamut in file names (e.g., SC001_SLOG3_SGAMUT3)
- Test workflow before principal photography
- Verify dailies look correct on set

### X-OCN Bitrate Selection Errors

**Symptom**:
- Compression artifacts in high-detail areas
- Banding in smooth gradients
- Color noise in shadows

**Root cause**:
- X-OCN LT used for high-motion or high-detail shots
- Insufficient bitrate for scene complexity
- Wrong quality level selected

**X-OCN quality guidelines**:
- **XT**: VFX, green screen, high motion, archival
- **ST**: Most narrative work, balanced quality/size
- **LT**: B-roll, behind-the-scenes, review copies

**Detection**:
- Visual inspection of high-detail areas
- Waveform monitor shows quantization in smooth gradients
- Vectorscope shows noise in saturated colors

**Solution**:
1. Use higher quality X-OCN (ST or XT)
2. Re-shoot if critical (not always possible)
3. Denoise in post (last resort)

**Prevention**:
- Decide on X-OCN quality before shoot
- Test compression artifacts with test charts
- Use ST for most work, XT for critical shots
- Reserve LT for non-critical content

### CineEI vs Custom Mode Confusion

**Symptom**:
- Different exposure behavior than expected
- ISO settings don't match traditional camera behavior
- Inconsistent noise levels across takes

**Root cause**:
- CineEI mode selected but treated as Custom mode
- EI adjustments expected to change gain (they don't)
- ISO gain structure misunderstood

**CineEI behavior**:
- Camera records at base ISO (800 or 3200) always
- EI changes monitoring LUT, not recorded signal
- Highlight headroom constant regardless of EI
- Shadow noise constant regardless of EI

**Custom mode behavior**:
- Traditional ISO gain applied
- ISO changes recorded signal
- Less highlight headroom at higher ISO
- More noise at higher ISO

**Detection**:
- Exposure doesn't change when EI adjusted
- Camera report shows "CineEI" mode
- Metadata shows base ISO (800/3200), not EI value

**Solution**:
1. Understand CineEI workflow before shoot
2. Use EI for monitoring brightness, not exposure
3. Expose based on waveform monitor, not EI value
4. Train camera team on CineEI behavior

**Prevention**:
- Decide on CineEI vs Custom before shoot
- Train camera assistants on CineEI workflow
- Document mode selection in camera report
- Use CineEI for S-Log3, Custom for S-Cinetone

## Best Practices (2024-2025)

### Exposure Strategy

1. **S-Log3 middle gray**:
   - 18% gray at CV 388/1023
   - Waveform monitor: 38-41% IRE with S-Log3 LUT
   - Skin tones at CV 450-500

2. **EI selection**:
   - EI 800 for most situations (optimal SNR)
   - EI 1600 for brighter monitoring
   - EI 3200 for low-light (2 stops less highlight headroom)

3. **Highlight protection**:
   - Reserve 2-3 stops above key face
   - Use false color to verify exposure
   - ETTR when possible (maximize signal without clipping)

4. **Monitoring**:
   - Apply S-Log3 to Rec.709 LUT on set
   - Verify with waveform monitor
   - Check skin tones on calibrated display

### Color Management

1. **Scene-referred workflow**:
   - Apply S-Log3 IDT first (ACES or Sony LUT)
   - Grade in scene-linear or log space
   - Apply display transform last (Rec.709, P3, HDR)

2. **Gamut selection**:
   - S-Gamut3 for maximum color capture
   - S-Gamut3.Cine for easier grading (recommended)
   - Document gamut in camera report

3. **Metadata preservation**:
   - Archive X-OCN with metadata
   - Keep camera reports with media
   - Test transcoding workflow before production

### Codec Selection

**X-OCN XT** (use for):
- Feature films (high-budget)
- VFX-heavy shots (green screen, CGI)
- Archival projects (future-proofing)
- High-motion or high-detail scenes

**X-OCN ST** (use for):
- Television drama
- Documentary with budget constraints
- Most narrative work (balanced quality/size)
- Web series with high quality requirements

**X-OCN LT** (use for):
- B-roll footage
- Behind-the-scenes content
- Review copies
- Non-critical shots

**ProRes 4444 XQ** (use for):
- Faster turnaround than X-OCN
- Easier dailies workflow
- Broadcast deliverables
- Commercial work with tight schedules

## Mini Glossary

- **S-Log3** [Sony Log 3rd generation]: Sony's logarithmic encoding for 14+ stop dynamic range with single-curve simplicity, 18% gray at CV 388/1023
- **X-OCN** [eXtended Tonal Range Original Camera Negative]: Sony's compressed RAW format with 16-bit linear quality, 3-5x smaller than uncompressed RAW
- **CineEI** [Cine Exposure Index]: Sony's ISO-independent exposure workflow where EI adjusts monitoring LUT but not recorded sensor gain
- **S-Gamut3.Cine** [Sony Gamut 3 Cinema]: Slightly smaller than S-Gamut3, designed for easier grading with P3-like behavior, recommended for most productions
- **VCE** [Venice Color Engine]: Sony's proprietary color processing pipeline designed for cinematic image quality with accurate skin tones
- **S-Cinetone**: Sony's built-in look designed for pleasing out-of-camera images with skin tone optimization and film-like contrast
- **Dual-base ISO**: Sony's dual-sensitivity sensor design with optimized circuitry for high (800/3200) and low (500/2500) ISO ranges
- **Base ISO**: Sensor's native sensitivity without gain applied, 800/3200 for VENICE 2/BURANO, 500/2500 for VENICE 1
- **EI** [Exposure Index]: ISO-like setting for exposure metering in CineEI mode, adjusts monitoring LUT but not recorded data
- **S-Gamut3**: Sony's widest color gamut, larger than Rec.2020, designed for maximum color capture
- **X-OCN XT** [eXtended Tone]: Highest quality X-OCN level, visually lossless, recommended for VFX and archival work
- **X-OCN ST** [Standard Tone]: Balanced quality and file size, recommended for most productions
- **X-OCN LT** [Lite Tone]: Maximum compression for longer record times, use for non-critical footage
- **Dynamic range**: Ratio between maximum signal and noise floor, Sony claims 16+ stops for VENICE 2 (measured ~15.5 stops)
- **SNR** [Signal-to-Noise Ratio]: Ratio of signal power to noise power, 54dB at 18% gray for VENICE 2 at ISO 800
- **Photosite**: Individual light-sensitive element on sensor, 4.19µm for VENICE 2/BURANO 8.6K sensor
