# 6.3 Mapping to HDR: Tone and Gamut Mapping

## Theory: HDR Mapping Fundamentals

### Tone Mapping Operators (TMOs)

**Tone Mapping**: Process of mapping high dynamic range (HDR) scene luminance to limited display range (SDR or low-nit HDR). Preserves visual appearance while adapting to display capability.

**Tone Mapping Goals**:
1. **Preserve Contrast**: Maintain scene contrast ratios
2. **Avoid Clipping**: Gracefully handle out-of-gamut luminance
3. **Maintain Detail**: Preserve shadow and highlight detail
4. **Natural Appearance**: Avoid artificial "HDR look"

**Tone Mapping Curve Types**:

1. **Sigmoid (S-Curve)**:
   ```
   L_out = 1 / (1 + exp(-k × (L_in - 0.5)))
   ```
   - Soft rolloff in highlights/shadows
   - Natural film-like appearance
   - Used in ACES RRT, film emulation

2. **Reinhard**:
   ```
   L_out = L_in / (1 + L_in)
   ```
   - Global tone mapping (single curve for entire image)
   - Simple, but can flatten contrast
   - Rarely used in professional workflows

3. **Filmic (Default ACES RRT)**:
   ```
   L_out = (A × L_in + B) × L_in / (L_in × (C × L_in + D) + E)
   ```
   - Highlight rolloff with mid-tone contrast preservation
   - Adjustable parameters (A-E) for creative control
   - Basis for many film emulation LUTs

4. **Perceptual Quantizer (PQ)**:
   ```
   L_out = P_max × ( ( (c + a) / (1 + a × (c + a)) )^b )^γ
   ```
   - Optimized for human perception (12-bit perceptual resolution)
   - Used in HDR10, Dolby Vision
   - Absolute luminance encoding

### Gamut Mapping Strategies

**Gamut Mapping**: Mapping colors from source gamut (e.g., Rec.2020) to destination gamut (e.g., Rec.709/P3) while preserving hue and minimizing saturation loss.

**Gamut Mapping Algorithms**:

1. **Clipping** (simplest):
   ```
   If RGB_outside_dest_gamut:
      Scale to nearest boundary
   ```
   - **Pros**: Simple, fast
   - **Cons**: Loses saturation, hue shifts
   - **Use Case**: Quick preview, real-time applications

2. **Soft Clip** (better):
   ```
   If RGB_outside_dest_gamut:
      Desaturate proportionally
      Preserve hue angle
   ```
   - **Pros**: Preserves hue, reduces clipping
   - **Cons**: Can cause pastel appearance
   - **Use Case**: SDR→Rec.709 conversion

3. **Chroma Reduction** (best):
   ```
   If RGB_outside_dest_gamut:
      Reduce chroma (C* in LCh)
      Preserve hue (h) and lightness (L*)
      Scale to gamut boundary
   ```
   - **Pros**: Best perceptual quality
   - **Cons**: Computationally expensive
   - **Use Case**: High-quality conversions (ACES, Dolby)

**Gamut Mapping in HDR**:
- **Rec.2020 → P3**: Reduce chroma for out-of-gamut colors (cyans, magentas)
- **Rec.2020 → Rec.709**: Significant chroma reduction (P3 vs. 709)
- **P3 → Rec.2020**: No mapping needed (P3 is subset of Rec.2020)

### Color Volume Mapping

**Color Volume**: 3D color space combining luminance (Y) and chromaticity (xy or u'v'). HDR color volumes are larger than SDR due to higher peak luminance.

**Color Volume Mapping Types**:

1. **Luminance-Dependent Gamut Mapping**:
   - Map highlights differently than midtones/shadows
   - Preserve saturation in bright areas (HDR effect)
   - Reduce saturation in dark areas (avoid posterization)

2. **ICtCp Color Space** ( perceptually uniform):
   ```
   I = Intensity (luminance)
   Ct = Chroma (red-green)
   Cp = Chroma (blue-yellow)
   ```
   - Used in Dolby Vision tone mapping
   - Better hue preservation than RGB

3. **JzAzBz Color Space** (2024 standard):
   ```
   J = Lightness (perceptual)
   az = Chroma (red-green)
   bz = Chroma (blue-yellow)
   ```
   - Designed for HDR/WCG (wide color gamut)
   - Better perceptual uniformity than ICtCp
   - Recommended for HDR tone mapping operators

**JzAzBz Advantages**:
- **Perceptual Uniformity**: Equal ΔE steps ≈ equal perceptual difference
- **HDR Support**: Designed for 0-10,000 nit range
- **Hue Linearity**: Preserves hue during gamut mapping
- **Adoption**: Growing in HDR tools (ACES, Dolby)

### SDR-to-HDR Mapping

**Challenge**: SDR has limited range (0-100 nits, Rec.709 gamut). HDR has extended range (0-1000+ nits, Rec.2020 gamut).

**SDR→HDR Strategies**:

1. **Simple Gain** (basic):
   ```
   L_HDR = L_SDR × Gain
   ```
   - **Gain**: 10× (100 → 1000 nits)
   - **Pros**: Simple, preserves relative contrast
   - **Cons**: Clipping (highlights exceed 1000 nits), banding

2. **Highlight Expansion** (better):
   ```
   L_HDR = Expand_highlights(L_SDR)
   ```
   - Stretch SDR highlights (70-100 nits → 500-1000 nits)
   - Add highlight detail (specular reflections, light sources)
   - **Requires**: Manual reconstruction (unreal highlights)

3. **AI Upscaling** (emerging, 2024-2025):
   ```
   L_HDR = AI_model(L_SDR, metadata)
   ```
   - Machine learning models predict HDR highlight detail
   - Training data: SDR/HDR pairs (same scene)
   - **Tools**: DaVinci Resolve Neural Engine, Topaz Video AI
   - **Quality**: Varies (can add artifacts if not trained well)

4. **Creative Grading** (recommended):
   ```
   L_HDR = Reconstruct_highlights(L_SDR, creative_intent)
   ```
   - Manually add highlight detail (windows, sky, lights)
   - Use power windows, qualifiers to isolate areas
   - Grade in HDR environment (1000-nit monitor)
   - **Quality**: Best (artistic control, no AI artifacts)

### HDR-to-SDR Mapping

**Challenge**: HDR has highlights (600-1000 nits) that SDR cannot display (100 nits max).

**HDR→SDR Strategies**:

1. **Simple Clip** (worst):
   ```
   L_SDR = min(L_HDR, 100 nits)
   ```
   - Clip all highlights >100 nits
   - **Result**: Loss of highlight detail, harsh appearance

2. **Hard Knee** (better):
   ```
   If L_HDR < 80 nits:
      L_SDR = L_HDR
   Else:
      L_SDR = 80 + (L_HDR - 80) × 0.2  # Tone map 80-1000 → 80-100
   ```
   - Preserve mid-tones (<80 nits)
   - Compress highlights (80-1000 → 80-100)
   - **Result**: Visible highlight rolloff

3. **Filmic Curve** (best):
   ```
   L_SDR = Filmic_TMO(L_HDR)
   ```
   - Soft rolloff (similar to ACES RRT)
   - Preserve mid-tone contrast
   - Natural highlight falloff
   - **Tools**: ACES RRT+ODT, film emulation LUTs

4. **Display-Referred Tone Mapping** (recommended):
   ```
   L_SDR = Display_Tone_Map(L_HDR, target_display)
   ```
   - Optimize for target SDR display (Rec.709, gamma 2.4)
   - Preserve creative intent (contrast, color)
   - **Tools**: DaVinci Resolve HDR→SDR mode, Dolby Vision trim passes

## Practice: HDR Grading Techniques

### Creative HDR Grading

**HDR Grading Philosophy**:
- **Preserve Scene Reality**: HDR should feel "real" (not exaggerated)
- **Add Selective Pop**: Use HDR for creative emphasis (sunsets, explosions, neon)
- **Maintain Mid-Tone Contrast**: Don't flatten mid-tones for highlight emphasis

**HDR Grading Workflow**:
```
1. Base Grade (Rec.709 Reference):
   - Grade as if for SDR (normal contrast)
   - Set middle gray at 10 nits
   - Normal color grading

2. HDR Expansion:
   - Add highlight detail (windows, sky, lights)
   - Push highlights to 600-1000 nits
   - Use power windows to isolate areas

3. HDR Refinement:
   - Adjust highlight rolloff (soft vs. hard)
   - Add selective saturation (neon signs, fire)
   - Validate on 1000-nit monitor

4. SDR Trim Pass:
   - Create SDR version (tone map 1000 → 100 nits)
   - Adjust contrast for SDR
   - Validate on Rec.709 monitor
```

**Example**: Sunset Scene
```
Original SDR:
- Sun: 80 nits (clipped highlight)
- Sky: 40-60 nits
- Shadows: 5-10 nits

HDR Grade:
- Sun: 800 nits (selective highlight expansion)
- Sky: 100-200 nits (HDR clouds)
- Shadows: 5-10 nits (unchanged)
- Color: Enhanced sunset colors (orange, red)

SDR Trim Pass:
- Sun: 90 nits (tone mapped from 800 nits)
- Sky: 50-70 nits (compressed from 100-200)
- Shadows: 5-10 nits (unchanged)
- Color: Match HDR grade (preserve intent)
```

### Highlight Roll-Off Control

**Highlight Roll-Off**: Transition from mid-tones to clipped highlights. Critical for natural HDR appearance.

**Roll-Off Types**:

1. **Hard Clip** (digital, harsh):
   ```
   L_out = min(L_in, 1000 nits)  # Abrupt clip
   ```
   - **Appearance**: Unnatural, video-like
   - **Use Case**: Security cameras, monitoring

2. **Linear Roll-Off** (better):
   ```
   If L_in > 800 nits:
      L_out = 800 + (L_in - 800) × 0.1  # Gradual compression
   ```
   - **Appearance**: Smoother, but still noticeable
   - **Use Case**: Sports, reality TV

3. **Filmic Roll-Off** (best):
   ```
   L_out = Filmic_Rolloff(L_in)
   ```
   - Sigmoid curve (S-curve)
   - **Appearance**: Film-like, natural
   - **Use Case**: Narrative film, commercial

**Roll-Off in Grading**:
- **Resolve**: Custom curve (curves node), highlight rolloff parameter
- **Baselight**: Highlight softness control
- **Nuke**: Grade node with custom roll-off expression

**Example**: Hard light source (sun, lamp)
```
No Roll-Off (Hard Clip):
- 800-1000 nits: Abrupt clip (white rectangle)
- Result: Distracting, unnatural

Soft Roll-Off (Filmic):
- 600-800 nits: Gradual compression
- 800-1000 nits: Smooth transition to white
- Result: Natural, pleasing

Hard Roll-Off (Linear):
- 900-950 nits: Sudden transition
- 950-1000 nits: Clipped
- Result: Noticeable but acceptable
```

### JzAzBz for HDR Color Management

**Why JzAzBz?**
- **Perceptual Uniformity**: Better than ICtCp for HDR
- **Hue Linearity**: Preserves hue during gamut mapping
- **Luminance Range**: Optimized for 0-10,000 nit range

**JzAzBz Conversion** (Python):
```python
import numpy as np

def XYZ_to_JzAzBz(X, Y, Z):
    # Convert XYZ to JzAzBz
    # (see Safdar et al. 2017 for full equations)
    # Simplified version:

    # Step 1: XYZ to LMS
    LMS = M_XYZ_to_LMS @ np.array([X, Y, Z])

    # Step 2: Apply PQ EOTF
    LMS_pq = pq_eotf(LMS)

    # Step 3: LMS to JzAzBz

    return JzAzBz  # [Jz, az, bz]
```

**JzAzBz Applications**:
1. **Gamut Mapping**:
   - Map Rec.2020 → P3 in JzAzBz (better hue preservation)
   - Reduce chroma (az, bz) while preserving Jz (lightness)

2. **HDR Quality Metrics**:
   - Measure ΔE (color difference) in JzAzBz
   - Evaluate tone mapping quality (HDR→SDR)

3. **Display Matching**:
   - Match appearance across displays (600 vs. 1000 nits)
   - Minimize perceptual differences

**Tools with JzAzBz**:
- **ACES 2.0**: JzAzBz color space for tone mapping
- **Dolby Vision**: ICtCp (similar to JzAzBz)
- **Python Libraries**: colour-science, PyOpenColorIO

### Tone Mapping with ACES

**ACES HDR Workflow**:
```
Scene Linear (ACES2065-1) → Grade (ACEScg) → LMT → ACESrender → ODT → Display
```

**HDR ODTs** (ACES 2.0):
- **ODT PQ 1000-nit**: HDR10 output
- **ODT PQ 4000-nit**: Dolby Cinema output
- **ODT HLG 1000-nit**: Broadcast HDR output

**ACES Tone Mapping** (RRT + ODT):
- **RRT**: Scene→display rendering (highlight rolloff, gamut mapping)
- **ODT**: Display-specific tone mapping (PQ 1000 nits, HLG 1000 nits)

**Custom Tone Mapping** (via LMT):
```python
# Custom LMT for creative HDR look
def custom_hdr_lmt(acescg):
    # Boost highlights
    acescg_hdr = np.where(acescg > 0.5, acescg ** 0.8, acescg)

    # Add selective saturation
    saturation_boost = 1.2
    acescg_hdr[:, 1:] *= saturation_boost  # Boost R, G channels

    return acescg_hdr
```

**Advantages of ACES for HDR**:
- **Single Master**: Grade once in ACEScg, deliver to all HDR formats
- **Consistency**: Same creative look across HDR10, HDR10+, Dolby Vision
- **Future-Proof**: Add new HDR ODTs as standards emerge

## Failure Modes: Common Mapping Issues

### Clipping

**Symptom**: Loss of highlight detail, white rectangles, "blown out" areas

**Causes**:
1. **Grading Too Bright**: Highlights exceed 1000 nits
   - Example: Sun at 2000 nits on 1000-nit mastering display
   - Result: Clip at 1000 nits (lost detail)

2. **Wrong EOTF**: HLG footage interpreted as PQ
   - Example: HLG 0.8 → PQ 0.8 (different luminance)
   - Result: Clipping (HLG 0.8 = 400 nits, PQ 0.8 = 600 nits)

3. **Display Limitation**: 600-nit display used for 1000-nit grade
   - Example: Grade on 600-nit monitor, deliver for 1000 nits
   - Result: 600-1000 nit range not visible (clipped on consumer display)

**Solution**:
- **Monitor Peak**: Use 1000-nit display for HDR10 grading
- **Check Histogram**: Ensure no clipping (use HDR scopes)
- **Target Baseline**: Grade for 600-800 nits (conservative for consumer displays)

### Banding

**Symptom**: Visible steps in gradients (sky, walls), posterization

**Causes**:
1. **Insufficient Bit Depth**: 8-bit for HDR
   - Example: Export HDR10 as 8-bit HEVC
   - Result: Severe banding (especially in darks)

2. **Aggressive Grading**: Large contrast adjustments
   - Example: Push shadows +2 stops (amplifies quantization)
   - Result: Banding in lifted shadows

3. **Heavy Compression**: Low bitrate HDR encoding
   - Example: 4K HDR10 at 20 Mbps (too low)
   - Result: Compression artifacts + banding

**Solution**:
- **Use 10-Bit Minimum**: Export HDR as 10-bit HEVC
- **Add Dither**: Apply dithering (noise) to mask banding
- **Bitrate**: Aim 40+ Mbps for 4K HDR (Netflix recommends 50+ Mbps)

### Hue Shifts

**Symptom**: Color changes during tone mapping, especially saturated colors

**Causes**:
1. **Poor Gamut Mapping**: Clipping instead of chroma reduction
   - Example: Rec.2020 → Rec.709 with simple clipping
   - Result: Hue shifts (cyan → blue, magenta → red)

2. **Wrong Color Space**: Grading in wrong primaries
   - Example: Grade in P3, deliver Rec.2020
   - Result: Out-of-gamut colors shift when rendered

3. **Saturation Boost in HDR**: Excessive saturation in highlights
   - Example: Boost saturation in 800-nit highlights
   - Result: Hue shifts (monitor gamut limits)

**Solution**:
- **Use Proper Gamut Mapping**: ICtCp or JzAzBz (preserve hue)
- **Grade in Target Color Space**: Rec.2020 for HDR10
- **Moderate Saturation**: Don't over-saturate highlights

### Unnatural HDR Appearance

**Symptom**: "HDR look" (everything glowing, hyper-real), eye fatigue

**Causes**:
1. **Excessive Highlight Pop**: Everything at 1000 nits
   - Example: Push entire scene to 600-1000 nits
   - Result: No contrast, unnatural appearance

2. **Wrong EOTF Parameters**: PQ with wrong mastering display
   - Example: Grade 4000-nit content, view on 600-nit display
   - Result: Tone mapping artifacts (harsh rolloff)

3. **Poor SDR→HDR Conversion**: Simple gain instead of highlight reconstruction
   - Example: SDR 100 nits × 10 = 1000 nits
   - Result: Flat HDR (no dynamic range expansion)

**Solution**:
- **Selective HDR**: Use HDR for creative emphasis only (sunsets, explosions)
- **Target Baseline**: Master for 600-1000 nits (not 4000 nits)
- **Manual Highlight Reconstruction**: Add realistic highlight detail (don't just boost gain)

## Mini Glossary: HDR Mapping Terminology

- **Tone Mapping Operator (TMO)**: Algorithm mapping HDR luminance to SDR or low-nit display. Types: Sigmoid, Reinhard, Filmic, Perceptual Quantizer.

- **Gamut Mapping**: Mapping colors from source gamut (Rec.2020) to destination gamut (Rec.709, P3). Preserves hue, reduces saturation for out-of-gamut colors.

- **Color Volume**: 3D color space combining luminance (nits) and chromaticity (xy or u'v'). HDR color volumes larger than SDR (higher peak luminance).

- **JzAzBz**: Perceptually uniform color space for HDR/WCG. Better than ICtCp for hue preservation. Designed for 0-10,000 nit range. Recommended for HDR tone mapping.

- **ICtCp**: Constant luminance color space (intensity, chroma). Used in Dolby Vision tone mapping. Similar to JzAzBz but less perceptually uniform.

- **Highlight Roll-Off**: Transition from mid-tones to clipped highlights. Types: Hard clip (abrupt), linear (gradual), filmic (smooth, S-curve).

- **Banding**: Visible steps in smooth gradients (sky, walls). Caused by insufficient bit depth, aggressive grading, heavy compression.

- **Hue Shift**: Unintended color change during tone mapping/gamut mapping. Caused by poor gamut mapping (clipping vs. chroma reduction).

- **Clipping**: Loss of highlight detail (white rectangles). Caused by exceeding display peak (1000 nits) or wrong EOTF.

- **Posterization**: Visible color steps (similar to banding). Caused by aggressive color grading or low bit depth.

- **Dithering**: Adding noise to mask banding/posterization. Applied in 8-bit → 10-bit conversions, heavy compression.

- **SDR→HDR**: Converting SDR (0-100 nits) to HDR (0-1000 nits). Strategies: Simple gain, highlight expansion, AI upscaling, creative grading.

- **HDR→SDR**: Converting HDR (0-1000 nits) to SDR (0-100 nits). Strategies: Hard clip, knee compression, filmic curve, display-referred tone mapping.

- **Selective HDR**: Creative use of HDR for specific elements (sunsets, explosions, neon). Not everything at 1000 nits.

- **ACESrender**: ACES 2.0 rendering pipeline (flexible tone mapping). Replaces fixed RRT, supports custom LMTs.
