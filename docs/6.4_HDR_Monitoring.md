# 6.4 HDR Monitoring and Calibration

## Theory: HDR Display Technologies

### OLED vs. LCD vs. Dual-Layer

**OLED (Organic Light-Emitting Diode)**:
- **Technology**: Self-emissive pixels (each pixel generates light)
- **Peak Luminance**: 700-1000 nits (consumer), 1000-2000 nits (professional)
- **Black Level**: 0.0005 nits (true black, infinite contrast)
- **Contrast Ratio**: ~1,000,000:1 (near-infinite)
- **Response Time**: <0.1 ms (instant, no motion blur)
- **Viewing Angles**: Excellent (180°, no color shift)
- **Burn-In Risk**: High (static images cause permanent damage)
- **HDR Examples**: LG OLED (consumer), Sony BVM-HX310 (professional)

**LCD (Liquid Crystal Display) + Local Dimming**:
- **Technology**: Backlight + LCD panel (local dimming zones)
- **Peak Luminance**: 600-4000 nits (mini-LED up to 4000 nits)
- **Black Level**: 0.05 nits (limited by backlight bleed)
- **Contrast Ratio**: 5,000:1 - 200,000:1 (depends on local dimming zones)
- **Response Time**: 2-10 ms (motion blur possible)
- **Viewing Angles**: Good (IPS panel), but contrast shifts off-axis
- **Burn-In Risk**: Low (no permanent burn-in)
- **HDR Examples**: Sony X95L (consumer), Dolby Pulsar (professional 4000-nit dual-layer)

**Dual-Layer LCD**:
- **Technology**: Two LCD panels (modulation + color layer)
- **Peak Luminance**: 1000-4000 nits (professional only)
- **Black Level**: 0.005 nits (better than single-layer LCD)
- **Contrast Ratio**: ~200,000:1 (approaching OLED)
- **Response Time**: Similar to LCD (5-10 ms)
- **Advantage**: No burn-in, high peak (4000 nits)
- **HDR Examples**: Dolby Pulsar (4000-nit professional monitor)

**Comparison Table**:

| Characteristic | OLED | LCD (Local Dimming) | Dual-Layer LCD |
|----------------|------|---------------------|----------------|
| **Peak Luminance** | 700-2000 nits | 600-4000 nits | 1000-4000 nits |
| **Black Level** | 0.0005 nits | 0.05 nits | 0.005 nits |
| **Contrast Ratio** | ~1,000,000:1 | 5,000-200,000:1 | ~200,000:1 |
| **Response Time** | <0.1 ms | 2-10 ms | 5-10 ms |
| **Burn-In Risk** | High | Low | Low |
| **Cost** | High | Medium | Very High |
| **Best For** | Critical review, cinema | Broadcast, general HDR | Dolby Vision mastering |

### Nits and Peak Luminance

**Nit (cd/m²)**: Unit of luminance (candelas per square meter). 1 nit = 1 cd/m².

**Peak Luminance Categories**:
- **SDR**: 100 nits (Rec.709 standard)
- **Entry HDR**: 300-600 nits (budget HDR TVs)
- **Standard HDR**: 1000 nits (HDR10, Dolby Vision target)
- **High HDR**: 2000-4000 nits (Dolby Cinema, premium HDR)
- **Ultra HDR**: 10,000 nits (PQ theoretical max, no current displays)

**Peak Luminance and Mastering**:
- **Grade for Target**: Master for peak luminance of target display (600-1000 nits for HDR10)
- **Conservative Approach**: Master for 600-800 nits (ensures compatibility)
- **Cinema Mastering**: 4000-nit Dolby Pulsar (for Dolby Vision cinema)

**Example Luminance Values**:
```
Candle flame: 10 nits
Office lighting: 100-500 nits
Overcast sky: 2000 nits
Clear sky: 10,000 nits
Sun: 1,000,000 nits (1 million)
```

### Black Level and Contrast Ratio

**Black Level**: Minimum luminance display can produce (darkest black).

**Black Level Categories**:
- **OLED**: 0.0005 nits (true black, emissive pixels off)
- **Dual-Layer LCD**: 0.005 nits (excellent backlight control)
- **High-End LCD**: 0.05 nits (local dimming zones)
- **Standard LCD**: 0.1-0.5 nits (backlight bleed)

**Contrast Ratio**: Ratio of peak luminance to black level.
```
Contrast Ratio = Peak Luminance / Black Level
```

**Examples**:
- **OLED**: 1000 nits / 0.0005 nits = 2,000,000:1
- **Dual-Layer LCD**: 1000 nits / 0.005 nits = 200,000:1
- **High-End LCD**: 1000 nits / 0.05 nits = 20,000:1
- **SDR Display**: 100 nits / 0.1 nits = 1,000:1

**Perceptual Impact**:
- **High Contrast**: Deepest blacks, HDR "pop" (OLED best)
- **Low Contrast**: Washed out blacks, less immersion (LCD limitation)

**Contrast Ratio Perception**:
- **Noticeable Difference**: 2× contrast ratio (1000:1 → 2000:1)
- **Significant Improvement**: 10× contrast ratio (1000:1 → 10,000:1)
- **Diminishing Returns**: >100,000:1 (OLED vs. dual-layer LCD)

## Practice: HDR Monitor Setup

### HDR Monitor Selection

**Professional Grading Monitors** (2024-2025):
1. **Sony BVM-HX310** (OLED, 31-inch):
   - **Peak**: 1000 nits (PQ), HLG supported
   - **Black Level**: 0.0005 nits (true black)
   - **Color**: Rec.2020 (98% coverage), P3/D65
   - **Price**: ~$25,000
   - **Best For**: Critical HDR grading, Dolby Vision

2. **Dolby Pulsar** (Dual-Layer LCD, 42-inch):
   - **Peak**: 4000 nits (PQ), Dolby Vision reference
   - **Black Level**: 0.005 nits (excellent for LCD)
   - **Color**: Rec.2020 (95% coverage), P3/D65
   - **Price**: ~$40,000
   - **Best For**: Dolby Vision mastering, cinema HDR

3. **ASUS ProArt PA32UCX** (Mini-LED, 32-inch):
   - **Peak**: 1000 nits (PQ), HLG supported
   - **Black Level**: 0.05 nits (good for LCD)
   - **Color**: Rec.2020 (95% coverage), P3/D65
   - **Price**: ~$2,000
   - **Best For**: Budget HDR grading, editing

**Selection Criteria**:
- **Budget**: Professional OLED ($25k) vs. Consumer OLED ($2-3k)
- **Peak Luminance**: 1000 nits (HDR10) vs. 4000 nits (Dolby Vision)
- **Panel Type**: OLED (best blacks) vs. LCD (no burn-in)
- **Color Gamut**: Rec.2020 coverage (95%+ recommended)

### Monitor Configuration

**Display Settings** (PQ/ST-2084):
1. **EOTF**: ST-2084 (PQ)
2. **Peak Luminance**: 1000 nits (or 4000 nits for Dolby)
3. **Black Level**: Auto (or 0.005 nits for OLED)
4. **Color Primaries**: Rec.2020 (or P3 for Dolby Cinema)
5. **White Point**: D65 (x=0.3127, y=0.3290)
6. **Gamma**: ST-2084 (PQ curve, not gamma 2.4)

**Display Settings** (HLG/B67):
1. **OOTF**: HLG (ARIB STD-B67)
2. **System Gamma**: Auto (adapts to display peak)
3. **Peak Luminance**: 1000 nits (system max)
4. **Color Primaries**: Rec.2020
5. **White Point**: D65
6. **Gamma**: Not applicable (HLG uses OOTF)

**OSD (On-Screen Display) Setup**:
- **Picture Mode**: HDR Mode (not Cinema/Standard)
- **Local Dimming**: High (for LCD, minimizes blooming)
- **Motion Enhance**: Off (causes artificial motion smoothing)
- **Color Temp**: Warm 2 (closest to D65)
- **Sharpness**: 0 (no artificial edge enhancement)

### Calibration Process

**Calibration Tools**:
1. **Calman** (Portrait Displays): Industry-standard calibration software
2. **LightSpace CMS**: Professional calibration (Hollywood standard)
3. **Chromasure**: Budget alternative (acceptable for basic calibration)

**Calibration Hardware**:
- **Pattern Generator**: Calman Pattern Generator (required for HDR)
- **Probe**: X-Rite i1 Display Pro (up to 1000 nits), Klein K10-A (up to 10,000 nits)
- **Cables**: HDMI 2.0b+ (18 Gbps for 4K HDR)

**PQ Calibration Steps**:
```
1. Pre-Calibration Check:
   - Warm up monitor: 30 minutes minimum
   - Reset to factory defaults
   - Verify HDMI 2.0b+ connection (4K/60Hz HDR)

2. EOTF Calibration (ST-2084):
   - Target: PQ curve (0-1000 nits)
   - Measure: 0-100% steps (21 points)
   - Adjust: Gamma/EOTF in OSD
   - Validate: ΔE < 2.0 (perceptible but acceptable)

3. Color Primaries Calibration:
   - Target: Rec.2020 (xy coordinates)
   - Measure: 100% saturated primaries (R, G, B)
   - Adjust: Color management system (CMS) in OSD
   - Validate: ΔE < 1.0 (imperceptible)

4. White Point Calibration:
   - Target: D65 (x=0.3127, y=0.3290)
   - Measure: 100% white (1000 nits)
   - Adjust: Color temp (warm 2 or custom)
   - Validate: ΔE < 1.0

5. Black Level Calibration:
   - Measure: 0% black (0 nits)
   - Adjust: Brightness control (OLED: auto)
   - Validate: Black level <0.01 nits (OLED <0.001 nits)

6. Verification:
   - Run HDR stripes test (10-100% luminance steps)
   - Check banding (should be minimal)
   - Validate skin tones (natural appearance)

7. Save 3D LUT:
   - Export calibration LUT (1D or 3D)
   - Load into grading software (Resolve, Baselight)
   - Re-validate: Apply LUT, check appearance
```

**Calibration Frequency**:
- **Professional OLED**: Monthly (drift due to burn-in compensation)
- **Consumer OLED**: Quarterly (acceptable drift)
- **LCD**: Quarterly (minimal drift)
- **Critical Production**: Weekly (Dolby Vision, theatrical release)

### HDR Monitor Validation

**Validation Tests**:
1. **HDR Stripes Test** (EOTF validation):
   - Purpose: Verify PQ curve accuracy (0-1000 nits)
   - Test Pattern: 10% luminance steps (0, 10, 20...100%)
   - Expected: Smooth steps, no banding
   - Pass Criteria: Each step distinct, ΔE < 2.0

2. **CLPL (Contrast Luminance Pattern Level)**:
   - Purpose: Validate peak luminance and black level
   - Test Pattern: Small white box (1000 nits) on black background
   - Expected: Peak at 1000 nits, black <0.01 nits
   - Pass Criteria: Contrast ratio >100,000:1 (OLED >1,000,000:1)

3. **Color Bars** (Rec.2020 validation):
   - Purpose: Verify color primary accuracy
   - Test Pattern: 100% saturated R, G, B (Rec.2020)
   - Expected: Match Rec.2020 xy coordinates
   - Pass Criteria: ΔE < 1.0 (imperceptible)

4. **Skin Tone Test** (perceptual validation):
   - Purpose: Check natural skin tone reproduction
   - Test Pattern: Various skin tones (light to dark)
   - Expected: Natural appearance, no hue shift
   - Pass Criteria: Subjective evaluation (colorist approval)

5. **Bandwidth Test** (HDMI 2.0b+ validation):
   - Purpose: Verify cable/hardware capability
   - Test Pattern: 4K/60Hz HDR, 10-bit, 4:2:2
   - Expected: Stable image, no dropouts
   - Pass Criteria: No artifacts, HDR metadata detected

**Validation Tools**:
- **Calman Verification**: Automated validation (HDR stripes, color bars)
- **Murideo Test Patterns**: 4K HDR test patterns (free download)
- **SMPTE Test Patterns**: Official SMPTE ST-2084 test patterns

**Common Validation Failures**:
1. **Peak Luminance Low**: Measured 800 nits (target 1000 nits)
   - **Cause**: ABL (automatic brightness limiter) activated
   - **Fix**: Disable ABL, reduce window size (10% white vs. 100% white)

2. **Color Shifts**: Rec.2020 green measured as yellow-green
   - **Cause**: Color space set to P3 instead of Rec.2020
   - **Fix**: Change color space to Rec.2020 in OSD

3. **Banding**: Visible steps in gradients
   - **Cause**: 8-bit signal instead of 10-bit, or aggressive grading
   - **Fix**: Use 10-bit signal, add dithering

4. **Metadata Not Detected**: HDR display shows SDR
   - **Cause**: HDMI cable not HDMI 2.0b+, or wrong port
   - **Fix**: Use certified HDMI 2.0b+ cable, use HDMI 2.0 port (not HDMI 1.4)

## Failure Modes: Common Monitoring Issues

### Wrong Nits (Peak Luminance)

**Symptom**: Grade looks too bright/dark on consumer displays, clipping, or washed out

**Causes**:
1. **Monitor Set to Wrong Peak**:
   - Example: Grade on 4000-nit monitor, deliver for 1000-nit consumer displays
   - Result: Grade too dark (consumer displays tone map 4000→1000 nits)

2. **ABL (Automatic Brightness Limiter) Activated**:
   - Example: Measure 100% white (full screen) → 600 nits (instead of 1000 nits)
   - Cause: Monitor reduces brightness to protect OLED (large bright areas)
   - Fix: Use small window patterns (10% white) for calibration

3. **Monitor Drift**:
   - Example: OLED calibrated to 1000 nits, now measures 800 nits
   - Cause: OLED brightness degradation (5% per 1000 hours)
   - Fix: Re-calibrate monthly, adjust brightness compensation

**Solution**:
- **Match Mastering Display**: Use 1000-nit display for HDR10 grading
- **Check ABL**: Disable ABL during calibration, use small windows (10% white)
- **Regular Calibration**: Calibrate monthly (professional OLED), quarterly (consumer)

### Tone Mapping on Monitor

**Symptom**: Grade changes when viewing conditions change, inconsistent appearance

**Causes**:
1. **Monitor EOTF Mismatch**:
   - Example: Grading in HLG, monitor set to PQ
   - Result: Wrong contrast (HLG 0.5 ≠ PQ 0.5)

2. **System Gamma Wrong** (HLG):
   - Example: HLG monitor with system gamma 1.0 (should be 1.2 for 1000 nits)
   - Result: Incorrect brightness (HLG designed for 1.2 system gamma)

3. **Tone Mapping Activated**:
   - Example: Consumer display tone mapping 1000→600 nits (built-in)
   - Result: Grade looks different (monitor altering image)

**Solution**:
- **Verify EOTF**: Check monitor OSD (PQ vs. HLG)
- **System Gamma**: Set to auto (HLG) or 1.2 (1000-nit target)
- **Disable Tone Mapping**: Use professional mode (disable consumer tone mapping)

### Incorrect EOTF

**Symptom**: Wrong contrast/tonality, image too dark or bright

**Causes**:
1. **Wrong EOTF Selected**:
   - Example: PQ footage viewed with HLG OOTF (or vice versa)
   - Result: Wrong contrast (PQ 0.58 = 10 nits, HLG 0.58 = ~400 nits)

2. **EOTF Calibration Drift**:
   - Example: PQ curve drifted (measured vs. target)
   - Cause: Gamma/EOTF settings changed
   - Fix: Re-calibrate EOTF using Calman

3. **Grading Software EOTF Wrong**:
   - Example: Resolve set to Rec.709 (gamma 2.4) instead of PQ
   - Result: Wrong rendering (grade not displayed correctly)

**Solution**:
- **Check EOTF**: Verify monitor OSD (PQ, HLG, gamma)
- **Calibrate EOTF**: Run PQ/HLG calibration (Calman)
- **Software Settings**: Match project settings to monitor (PQ/HLG)

### Calibration Drift

**Symptom**: Monitor changes over time, grade shifts

**Causes**:
1. **OLED Burn-In Compensation**:
   - Example: OLED reduces brightness in areas with static images
   - Cause: Burn-in compensation (shifts brightness over time)
   - Fix: Run panel refresh (OLED maintenance mode)

2. **LED Aging** (LCD):
   - Example: Backlight LEDs dim over time (10% brightness loss after 10,000 hours)
   - Cause: LED aging (gradual brightness reduction)
   - Fix: Increase brightness compensation, re-calibrate

3. **Temperature Effects**:
   - Example: Monitor brighter when cold (after power on)
   - Cause: OLED/LCD efficiency varies with temperature
   - Fix: Warm up monitor 30 minutes before grading

**Solution**:
- **Regular Calibration**: Monthly (professional), quarterly (consumer)
- **Panel Refresh**: Run OLED panel refresh monthly (prevents burn-in)
- **Warm Up**: Turn on monitor 30 minutes before grading (stabilize temperature)

## Mini Glossary: HDR Monitoring Terminology

- **Nit (cd/m²)**: Unit of luminance (candelas per square meter). 1 nit = 1 cd/m². SDR = 100 nits, HDR = 600-10,000 nits.

- **Peak Luminance**: Maximum brightness display can produce (nits). HDR10 = 1000 nits (typical), Dolby Vision = 4000 nits (cinema).

- **Black Level**: Minimum luminance display can produce (darkest black). OLED = 0.0005 nits (true black), LCD = 0.05 nits (backlight bleed).

- **Contrast Ratio**: Ratio of peak luminance to black level. OLED = ~1,000,000:1, LCD = 20,000-200,000:1.

- **OLED**: Organic Light-Emitting Diode display (self-emissive pixels). Best blacks, instant response, high contrast. Burn-in risk.

- **LCD**: Liquid Crystal Display (backlight + LCD panel). Local dimming improves contrast (600-4000 nits). No burn-in risk.

- **Dual-Layer LCD**: Two LCD panels (modulation + color layer). 1000-4000 nits, near-OLED contrast. Used in Dolby Pulsar.

- **ABL (Automatic Brightness Limiter)**: Monitor feature reducing brightness for large bright areas (protects OLED). Can affect calibration.

- **Calman**: Professional calibration software (Portrait Displays). Industry standard for HDR monitor calibration.

- **LightSpace CMS**: High-end calibration software (used in Hollywood). Advanced 3D LUT creation.

- **HDR Stripes**: Test pattern validating EOTF (0-100% luminance steps). Used for calibration verification.

- **CLPL (Contrast Luminance Pattern Level)**: Test pattern validating peak luminance and black level (small white box on black).

- **3D LUT**: 3D lookup table (RGB → RGB'). Stores calibration corrections. Loaded into grading software for consistent display.

- **EOTF (Electro-Optical Transfer Function)**: Signal → display luminance mapping. PQ EOTF (ST-2084) for HDR10.

- **OOTF (Opto-Optical Transfer Function)**: Scene light → signal → display light mapping. HLG OOTF (B67) for broadcast HDR.

- **System Gamma**: Display adaptation factor for HLG. Scales scene to display capability (1.2 for 1000 nits).

- **Burn-In**: Permanent image retention on OLED displays (static images cause pixel degradation). Mitigated by panel refresh, pixel shift.

- **Panel Refresh**: OLED maintenance mode (compensates burn-in, recalibrates pixels). Run monthly.

- **HDMI 2.0b+**: Required for 4K HDR (18 Gbps bandwidth). Supports 4K/60Hz, 10-bit, HDR metadata.
