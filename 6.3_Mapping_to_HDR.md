# 6.3 Mapping to HDR

## Theory: SDR-to-HDR Conversion and Tone Mapping

### Inverse Tone Mapping Fundamentals

**Challenges of SDR to HDR Conversion:**

Converting SDR [Standard Dynamic Range] content to HDR [High Dynamic Range] is fundamentally an interpretive process rather than a precise technical conversion. SDR content contains limited dynamic range (approximately 6-8 stops) and limited color gamut (Rec.709), while HDR offers expanded range (10+ stops) and wider color gamut (Rec.2020).

**Information Limitations:**

SDR source material has inherent limitations:
- **Clipped Highlights:** Pure white (code value 1023 in 10-bit) contains no recoverable detail
- **Crushed Shadows:** Pure black (code value 0) contains no detail
- **Limited Color Gamut:** Rec.709 encompasses ~36% of Rec.2020
- **Compressed Dynamic Range:** Limited luminance resolution in extreme ranges

**Mathematical Impossibility:**

No transform can recover information that doesn't exist in the source:
- Clipped highlights cannot be reconstructed
- Crushed shadows cannot be recovered
- Out-of-gamut colors cannot be accurately generated
- Texture detail in compressed ranges cannot be created

**Interpretive Nature:**

HDR conversion is inherently creative:
- Artist determines highlight distribution
- Color gamut expansion requires aesthetic choices
- Contrast adjustments affect creative intent
- Multiple valid approaches for same source

### Tone Mapping Operators

**Forward Tone Mapping (HDR to SDR):**

Well-established process for displaying HDR content on SDR displays:
- Preserve overall contrast while compressing range
- Maintain perceived brightness relationships
- Protect skin tone reproduction
- Preserve creative intent as much as possible

**Inverse Tone Mapping (SDR to HDR):**

Less defined process requiring artistic interpretation:

**Simple Expansion:**
```
L_HDR = L_SDR^γ
```
Where γ < 1.0 expands dynamic range, but can introduce artifacts and unnatural appearance.

**Adaptive Methods:**
- Analyze image characteristics
- Identify highlight and shadow regions
- Apply expansion based on local content
- Preserve color relationships
- Maintain natural appearance

**Advanced Algorithms:**

**Deep Learning Approaches:**
- Train neural networks on SDR-HDR pairs
- Learn to predict plausible HDR detail
- Generate texture in clipped regions
- Expand color gamut intelligently
- Risk: Generate unrealistic content

**Traditional Image Processing:**
- Edge-aware filtering for local adaptation
- Multi-scale tone mapping inversion
- Contrast-limited expansion
- Gamut mapping algorithms
- Risk: Processing artifacts and unnatural appearance

### Highlight Rolloff Reconstruction

**The Challenge:**

SDR hard clip becomes HDR rolloff:

**SDR Representation:**
- Highlights clip at 100 nits (Rec.709)
- No detail above clip point
- Color may desaturate near clip

**HDR Reconstruction:**
- Create plausible rolloff curve
- Generate texture in clipped areas
- Maintain color in highlights
- Avoid artificial appearance

**Reconstruction Strategies:**

**Conservative Approach:**
- Gentle rolloff starting at 80-90% of SDR peak
- Preserve some SDR contrast
- Limited extension to 200-400 nits
- Maintain natural appearance
- Use for subtle HDR enhancement

**Aggressive Approach:**
- Extend highlights to 1000+ nits
- Generate significant highlight detail
- Create strong HDR impact
- Risk of artificial appearance
- Use for dramatic HDR effect

**Creative Decisions:**
- Where should rolloff begin?
- How rapidly should brightness decay?
- What peak brightness is appropriate?
- Should color be preserved or desaturated?
- Does creative intent support HDR expansion?

### Color Gamut Expansion

**Gamut Mapping Challenge:**

Rec.709 to Rec.2020 expansion involves:
- **Primary Colors:** Red, green, blue primary coordinates
- **Secondary Colors:** Cyan, magenta, yellow mixtures
- **Skin Tones:** Critical flesh reproduction accuracy
- **Saturation:** Color intensity at different luminance levels

**Gamut Expansion Strategies:**

**Preserve Skin Tones:**
- Keep flesh colors within original appearance
- Expand saturation in non-skin areas
- Maintain natural skin tone reproduction
- Critical for viewer acceptance

**Selective Expansion:**
- Expand saturated colors more than neutral colors
- Preserve hue while increasing chroma
- Consider luminance-dependent saturation
- Maintain color harmony

**Conservative Approach:**
- Expand only obviously saturated colors
- Keep most colors near original appearance
- Maintain subtle, natural enhancement
- Minimize risk of artificial appearance

**Aggressive Approach:**
- Expand entire color gamut significantly
- Increase saturation across all colors
- Create strong HDR color impact
- Risk of cartoonish or artificial appearance

## Practice: HDR Grading from SDR Sources

### Preparation and Analysis

**Source Assessment:**

1. **Dynamic Range Analysis:**
   - Evaluate histogram for clipping
   - Identify clipped highlights
   - Identify crushed shadows
   - Assess recoverable detail

2. **Color Analysis:**
   - Check color saturation levels
   - Identify out-of-gamut colors
   - Assess skin tone reproduction
   - Evaluate color relationships

3. **Technical Quality:**
   - Check for compression artifacts
   - Identify noise in shadows
   - Evaluate sharpening/halos
   - Assess overall image quality

**Decision Process:**

**Suitable for HDR Conversion:**
- Well-exposed SDR with minimal clipping
- Good dynamic range utilization
- Clean technical quality
- Creative intent supports HDR enhancement

**Challenging for HDR Conversion:**
- Heavy highlight clipping
- Crushed shadows with no detail
- Excessive compression artifacts
- Limited creative benefit from HDR

**Poor HDR Candidate:**
- Severe clipping throughout
- Low-bitrate sources
- Heavy processing artifacts
- Creative intent doesn't support HDR

### Manual Grading Workflow

**Step 1: Project Setup**

**Color-Managed Workflow:**
```
DaVinci Resolve:
- Color Science: DaVinci YRGB Color Managed or ACES
- Timeline Color Space: Rec.2020
- Gamma: ST.2084 (PQ) for HDR output
- Input Color Space: Rec.709 Gamma 2.4
```

**ACES Workflow:**
```
Project Settings:
- Color Science: ACEScct
- Working Space: ACEScct
- Input Transform: Rec.709 ODT (to convert SDR to ACES)
- Output Transform: Rec.2020+P3 1000-nit ST.2084 ODT
```

**Step 2: Initial Grade in SDR Space**

1. **Color Correction:** Fix color balance and issues
2. **Contrast Adjustment:** Optimize SDR contrast
3. **Saturation:** Set appropriate color intensity
4. **Skin Tones:** Ensure accurate flesh reproduction
5. **Overall Look:** Establish creative direction

**Step 3: HDR Expansion**

**Dynamic Range Expansion:**
- Use custom curves to expand highlights
- Create gentle rolloff from SDR peak
- Extend to 400-1000 nits depending on content
- Preserve mid-tone contrast
- Maintain shadow detail

**Selective Expansion Strategies:**

**Window/ skies:**
- Expand to high brightness (600-1000 nits)
- Generate plausible highlight detail
- Maintain color in sky gradients
- Create HDR impact for exteriors

**Interior scenes:**
- Moderate expansion (200-400 nits)
- Maintain realistic indoor appearance
- Highlight practical light sources
- Preserve mood and atmosphere

**Night scenes:**
- Careful expansion to maintain night feeling
- Highlight actual light sources only
- Keep shadows dark but detailed
- Preserve creative intent

**Step 4: Color Enhancement**

**Gamut Expansion:**
- Increase saturation selectively
- Preserve skin tone accuracy
- Expand saturated colors more than neutrals
- Maintain color harmony
- Test on various displays

**Color Volume Considerations:**
- Not all colors available at all brightness levels
- Maintain color consistency across luminance
- Test on P3-limited displays
- Verify Rec.2020 colors don't clip on consumer displays
- Preserve color relationships

**Step 5: Refinement and Validation**

**Highlight Refinement:**
- Check for clipping on target displays
- Verify highlight rolloff appearance
- Test on OLED and LCD displays
- Adjust for natural appearance
- Maintain HDR impact without artificiality

**Shadow Refinement:**
- Maintain shadow detail
- Verify black level appropriate for content
- Test shadow detail visibility
- Ensure noise not excessive
- Preserve creative intent

**Final Validation:**
- Test on mastering display (1000-nit)
- Test on consumer displays (600-4000 nits)
- Verify SDR compatibility if needed
- Check various display technologies
- Validate creative intent preserved

## Failure Modes and Troubleshooting

### Blowout Highlights

**Symptoms:**
- Complete loss of highlight detail
- Hard clip at peak brightness
- Colorful highlights (rainbow edges)
- Harsh, unnatural appearance

**Root Causes:**
- Overly aggressive highlight expansion
- No rolloff applied to highlights
- Pushing highlights beyond display capability
- Ignoring source clipping limitations

**Solutions:**
1. Analyze source for actual clipping
2. Apply gentle highlight rolloff
3. Limit peak brightness expansion
4. Reserve headroom for genuine highlights
5. Test on target displays before finalizing

**Prevention:**
- Start with conservative expansion
- Gradually increase HDR impact
- Test frequently on reference displays
- Protect skin tones and critical highlights
- Maintain plausible highlight relationships

### Lost Shadow Detail

**Symptoms:**
- Crushed shadows with no detail
- Black level too high
- Noise visible in dark areas
- Loss of texture and detail

**Root Causes:**
- Overly aggressive shadow crushing
- Expanding contrast too aggressively
- Not protecting shadow detail
- Display black level limitations

**Solutions:**
1. Analyze source shadow detail
2. Protect shadows during expansion
3. Use noise reduction judiciously
4. Test on OLED displays (true black)
5. Verify shadow detail on various displays

**Best Practices:**
- Maintain shadow detail from source
- Use subtle shadow enhancement
- Test on both OLED and LCD
- Consider noise in dark areas
- Preserve texture and detail

### Wrong Gamma Curve

**Symptoms:**
- Incorrect overall brightness
- Mid-tones too bright or dark
- Contrast appears wrong
- Unnatural image appearance

**Root Causes:**
- Incorrect transfer function selected
- Confusing PQ and HLG approaches
- Inverse gamma applied incorrectly
- Color space mismatch

**Diagnosis:**
1. Verify project color management settings
2. Confirm transfer function matches target format
3. Check input color space matches source
4. Test on reference HDR display
5. Compare with reference material

**Solutions:**
- Reconfigure color management correctly
- Use appropriate transfer function (PQ for HDR10, HLG for broadcast)
- Verify color space conversions
- Test on target display types
- Document settings for reproducibility

### Color Distortion

**Symptoms:**
- Unnatural color appearance
- Skin tone inaccuracies
- Oversaturated colors
- Color shifts between displays

**Root Causes:**
- Overly aggressive gamut expansion
- Not protecting skin tones during expansion
- Color saturation too high
- Out-of-gamut colors clipping

**Solutions:**
1. Moderate color gamut expansion
2. Protect skin tone accuracy
3. Test on P3-limited displays
4. Maintain color harmony
5. Verify on various display types

**Best Practices:**
- Prioritize skin tone accuracy
- Expand saturated colors selectively
- Test color decisions on multiple displays
- Maintain natural appearance
- Preserve color relationships

## Mini Glossary

**Tone mapping** - Process of adapting dynamic range from source to target, critical for SDR-to-HDR conversion where highlights must be reconstructed.

**Highlight rolloff** - Gradual transition from mid-tones to peak brightness, critical for natural HDR appearance without harsh clipping.

**Inverse tone mapping** - Process of expanding SDR dynamic range to HDR, inherently interpretive due to limited source information.

**Color gamut expansion** - Process of extending colors from limited gamut (Rec.709) to wider gamut (Rec.2020), requiring aesthetic decisions.

**Clipping** - Loss of detail where signal reaches maximum value (white) or minimum value (black), unrecoverable through processing.

**Dynamic range** - Ratio between brightest and darkest reproducible values, approximately 6-8 stops for SDR, 10+ stops for HDR.

**Creative intent** - Artistic vision and aesthetic goals for imagery, should guide HDR conversion decisions rather than purely technical considerations.

**SDR** [Standard Dynamic Range] - Traditional video with limited dynamic range (~100 nits peak) and Rec.709 color gamut.

**HDR** [High Dynamic Range] - Extended dynamic range video (1000+ nits peak) with Rec.2020 color gamut and appropriate transfer functions.

**Rec.709** - SDR color gamut standard for HDTV, encompassing approximately 36% of Rec.2020 gamut.

**Rec.2020** - HDR color gamut standard for UHDTV, encompassing much wider color space than Rec.709.

**Gamut mapping** - Process of converting colors from one color space to another, critical for maintaining color appearance during SDR-to-HDR conversion.

**Color volume** - Combination of color gamut and luminance, describing available colors at different brightness levels.

**Metadata** - Information embedded in HDR content describing mastering display characteristics and content light levels, critical for proper HDR rendering.

**Mastering display** - Professional reference display used for HDR grading, meeting specific technical criteria for accuracy and performance.

**Consumer display** - Typical home HDR display with varying capabilities, important testing target for HDR content.

**Grade** - Creative color correction and adjustment process, determining final appearance of imagery.

**Compatibility** - Ability of content to display appropriately on various devices, critical consideration for HDR content with SDR fallback requirements.
