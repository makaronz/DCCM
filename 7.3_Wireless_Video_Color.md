# 7.3 Wireless Video and Color Accuracy

## Theory

### Wireless Transmission

[Wireless video] [Transmission of video signals via radio frequency without physical cables] has revolutionized on-set monitoring but introduces significant challenges for color accuracy. Understanding the transmission process is critical for maintaining color fidelity:

**RF Bands:**
- **5GHz unlicensed**: Most common, crowded spectrum, susceptible to interference from WiFi, Bluetooth, other wireless devices
- **60GHz V-band**: Higher bandwidth, shorter range, less interference, line-of-sight required
- **2.4GHz**: Avoid for critical video, extremely crowded, poor bandwidth

**Transmission Methods:**
- **Compressed**: Most systems use H.264/H.265 compression to fit within available bandwidth
- **Visually lossless**: High-quality compression where artifacts are imperceptible under normal viewing
- **Uncompressed**: Rare, requires massive bandwidth (>3Gbps for 4K), extremely limited range

### Compression Effects on Color

Wireless systems typically compress video signals, which affects color accuracy in several ways:

**Chroma Subsampling:**
- Most wireless systems use 4:2:0 subsampling to reduce bandwidth
- Color resolution is halved both horizontally and vertically
- Fine color details (edges, textures) are most affected
- Skin tones may appear slightly soft or smeared

**Bitrate Limitations:**
- Wireless bandwidth is finite and shared
- Lower bitrates = more compression artifacts
- Color banding in gradients is common at low bitrates
- Macro-blocking occurs during motion or complex scenes

**Compression Artifacts:**
- **Blocking**: Square artifacts in flat color areas
- **Banding**: Visible steps in smooth gradients
- **Color smearing**: Color bleeding across edges
- **Noise reduction artifacts**: Loss of fine texture detail

### Latency Considerations

[Latency] [Time delay between signal transmission and reception] is critical for real-time monitoring:

**Acceptable Latency:**
- **Direct monitoring**: <1 frame (41ms at 24fps)
- **Focus pulling**: <2 frames (83ms at 24fps)
- **Client video village**: <4 frames (166ms at 24fps)
- **Remote review**: 1-2 seconds acceptable

**Factors Affecting Latency:**
- Compression algorithm complexity
- Transmission distance
- Signal processing overhead
- Error correction and retransmission

### Maintaining Color Accuracy

**Best Practices:**

1. **Apply LUTs Before Transmission**
   - Use Teradek COLR or similar integrated LUT box
   - Critical: color grading on uncompressed signal
   - All stakeholders see same graded look
   - Avoid applying LUTs at receiver end

2. **Minimize Compression**
   - Use highest quality setting available
   - Accept shorter range for better quality
   - Test compression artifacts with test patterns
   - Monitor for banding in gradients

3. **Optimize Antenna Placement**
   - Line-of-sight between transmitter and receiver
   - Elevate antennas above obstructions
   - Use directional antennas for long range
   - Avoid metal surfaces and interference sources

4. **Manage Spectrum**
   - Use spectrum analyzer to find clean frequency
   - Avoid crowded WiFi channels
   - Monitor for interference throughout shoot
   - Have backup frequencies ready

5. **Signal Redundancy**
   - Wired backup to critical monitors
   - Multiple receivers for redundancy
   - Record uncompressed signal at camera
   - Test failover procedures

**Workflow Integration:**

*On-Set Grading with Wireless:*
- Camera → DIT cart (wired) → LUT box → COLR transmitter
- Receiver 1: Reference monitor (DIT, Director, DP)
- Receiver 2: Video village (Script, Continuity)
- Receiver 3: Client monitoring
- All receivers see identical graded image

*Multi-Camera Wireless:*
- Separate transmitter per camera
- Different frequencies for each camera
- Video switcher at receiver location
- LUT matching across all cameras
- Coordination for frequency assignments

**HDR Workflows:**

*Wireless HDR Challenges:*
- HDR metadata must be preserved through transmission
- Not all wireless systems support HDR metadata
- SDR downconversion may be necessary
- Test HDR metadata preservation thoroughly

*Recommended HDR Wireless Setup:*
- Teradek COLR with HDR metadata enabled
- HDR-compatible reference monitor at receiver
- Verify HDR metadata is received correctly
- Have SDR downconversion as backup

## Failure Modes

### Compression Artifacts

**Symptoms:**
- Visible blocks in flat color areas (walls, sky)
- Color banding in gradients (sunset, dim lighting)
- Color smearing across sharp edges
- Loss of fine texture detail

**Causes:**
- Insufficient bandwidth for signal complexity
- Too many receivers sharing transmitter
- Long transmission distance
- Interference reducing effective bandwidth

**Mitigation:**
- Reduce resolution if needed (1080p vs 4K)
- Increase transmitter power (if available)
- Improve antenna placement and line-of-sight
- Reduce number of receivers
- Test with worst-case scenes (fine detail, motion)

### Signal Dropouts

**Symptoms:**
- Complete signal loss (black screen)
- Frozen frame during transmission
- Audio dropouts
- Intermittent signal interruptions

**Causes:**
- Physical obstruction between transmitter and receiver
- Interference from other wireless devices
- Exceeding transmission range
- Multipath interference (signal reflections)

**Mitigation:**
- Maintain clear line-of-sight
- Use spectrum analyzer to avoid interference
- Add repeaters for long distances
- Employ antenna diversity (multiple antennas)
- Have wired backup ready

### Color Shifts

**Symptoms:**
- Colors appear different on wireless vs wired
- Skin tones look wrong (too red/yellow/blue)
- Color saturation changes
- White balance appears incorrect

**Causes:**
- Wrong color space conversion in wireless system
- Chroma subsampling artifacts
- Incorrect LUT application
- Color space mismatch between transmitter and receiver

**Mitigation:**
- Document color space throughout signal chain
- Test wireless vs wired before critical work
- Apply LUTs before transmission, not after
- Use 10-bit color to reduce banding
- Verify color space settings match on all devices

### Bandwidth Issues

**Symptoms:**
- Frame rate drops (stuttering video)
- Resolution suddenly decreases
- Compression artifacts worsen
- Increased latency

**Causes:**
- Too many devices sharing wireless spectrum
- Long transmission distance
- Obstacles reducing signal strength
- Multiple receivers exceeding transmitter capacity

**Mitigation:**
- Calculate required bandwidth with headroom
- Use 60GHz systems for short-range, high-bandwidth needs
- Reduce number of receivers
- Upgrade to higher-capacity transmitter
- Use wired connections where possible

## Mini Glossary

- **[Wireless video]**: Transmission of video signals via radio frequency without physical cables, enabling mobility and flexibility on set.

- **[Compression]**: Reduction of video data size by removing redundant or less important information, inevitable in wireless transmission due to bandwidth limitations.

- **[Latency]**: Time delay between signal transmission and reception, critical for real-time monitoring applications.

- **[Bandwidth]**: Maximum data rate available for transmission, typically measured in megabits per second (Mbps), determines maximum quality and resolution.

- **[Artifact]**: Visible degradation in image quality caused by compression or transmission errors, includes blocking, banding, and color smearing.

- **[Signal drop]**: Complete or partial loss of video signal during wireless transmission, caused by interference, distance, or obstructions.

- **[Line of sight]**: Unobstructed direct path between transmitter and receiver antennas, critical for reliable wireless transmission.

- **[Frequency spectrum]**: Range of radio frequencies available for transmission, shared with other wireless devices, susceptible to interference.

- **[Multipath interference]**: Signal degradation caused by radio waves reflecting off surfaces and arriving at receiver at slightly different times.

- **[Visually lossless]**: Compression level where artifacts are imperceptible under normal viewing conditions despite actual data loss.
