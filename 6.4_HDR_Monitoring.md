# 6.4 HDR Monitoring

## Theory: HDR Display Technologies and Characteristics

### OLED (Organic Light Emitting Diode)

**Technology Overview:**

OLED displays use organic compounds that emit light when electrical current is applied, enabling each pixel to produce its own light without requiring a backlight.

**Key Characteristics:**

**True Black Performance:**
- Perfect black level: 0.0005 nits or lower (effectively infinite contrast)
- Self-emissive pixels turn off completely for black
- No backlight bleeding or light leakage
- Superior shadow detail visibility
- Ideal for HDR content with dark scenes

**Peak Brightness:**
- Typical consumer: 600-800 nits sustained
- High-end consumer: 1000-1500 nits sustained
- Professional mastering: 1000 nits typical
- Limitations: ABL (Automatic Brightness Limiter) reduces peak with large bright areas

**Color Performance:**
- Wide color gamut: >90% P3, >70% Rec.2020
- Excellent color accuracy
- Consistent color off-axis
- No color shift at viewing angles

**Viewing Angles:**
- Near-perfect off-axis performance
- No contrast or color shift at extreme angles
- Suitable for client review environments

**Response Time:**
- Microsecond-level response
- No motion blur
- Excellent for fast-moving content

**ABL (Automatic Brightness Limiter):**

OLED displays reduce peak brightness when large areas of the screen are bright to prevent burn-in and manage power consumption.

**ABL Characteristics:**
- Small bright areas: Full peak brightness available (e.g., 1000 nits for 2% window)
- Large bright areas: Reduced peak brightness (e.g., 200-400 nits for 100% window)
- Content-dependent: ABL varies based on image brightness distribution
- Display-specific: Different manufacturers implement ABL differently

**Implications for HDR:**
- Test content with various brightness distributions
- Verify appearance on full-screen white vs small highlights
- Understand ABL behavior for accurate assessment
- Consider ABL when evaluating HDR impact

### LCD (Liquid Crystal Display) with Local Dimming

**Technology Overview:**

LCD displays use a backlight system (LED Mini-LED, FALD) with liquid crystal shutters, employing local dimming zones to improve contrast and HDR performance.

**Key Characteristics:**

**Peak Brightness:**
- Consumer LCD: 1000-4000 nits peak
- Professional: 1000-2000 nits typical
- Higher sustained brightness than OLED
- Better for bright room environments

**Contrast Performance:**
- Native contrast: ~5000:1 (typical)
- Effective contrast with local dimming: >1,000,000:1 claimed
- Zone-dependent: Contrast depends on local dimming zones
- Blooming/halo effect around bright objects on dark backgrounds

**Local Dimming Zones:**

**FALD (Full Array Local Dimming):**
- Premium displays: 500-2000+ zones
- Mid-range displays: 100-500 zones
- Each zone independently controlled for brightness
- More zones = better HDR rendering, less blooming

**Zone Limitations:**
- Blooming: Bright areas bleed into adjacent dark zones
- Halo effect: Visible halos around bright objects
- Limited precision: Zone boundaries visible in high-contrast scenes
- Content-dependent: Performance varies with scene characteristics

**Black Level:**
- Minimum luminance: 0.005-0.05 nits (dependent on local dimming)
- Not true black like OLED
- Zone-dependent: Black level varies with local dimming activity
- Visible in letterboxed content

**Color Performance:**
- Wide color gamut: >90% P3, >70% Rec.2020 typical
- Quantum Dot enhancement improves color volume
- Good color accuracy when calibrated
- Some color shift off-axis (improving with newer panels)

**Viewing Angles:**
- VA panels: Moderate viewing angle, some contrast/color shift off-axis
- IPS panels: Better viewing angles, lower native contrast
- Improving with newer technologies
- Not as good as OLED for off-axis viewing

**Response Time:**
- Millisecond-level response
- Some motion blur possible
- Improving with faster panels
- Generally good enough for most content

### Quantum Dot Technology

**Technology Overview:**

Quantum Dot enhancement improves color performance by providing purer red and green primaries.

**Benefits:**
- Wider color gamut than traditional LCD
- Improved color volume
- Better color accuracy
- Higher peak brightness capability

**Implementation:**
- Quantum Dot enhancement film (QDEF) in LCD panels
- Combined with local dimming for premium HDR performance
- Used in high-end consumer displays

### HDR Display Comparison

| Feature | OLED | LCD (Local Dimming) |
|---------|------|-------------------|
| **Black Level** | Perfect (infinite contrast) | Good (0.005-0.05 nits) |
| **Peak Brightness** | 600-1500 nits | 1000-4000 nits |
| **Contrast** | Infinite | Zone-dependent |
| **Viewing Angles** | Perfect | Good (IPS) to Fair (VA) |
| **ABL** | Yes (aggressive) | No (minimal) |
| **Blooming** | None | Visible with high contrast |
| **Color Gamut** | Excellent | Excellent (with QD) |
| **Best For** | Dark rooms, cinematic | Bright rooms, bright HDR |
| **Professional Use** | Color grading preferred | Client review acceptable |

### Calibration Procedures

**Pre-Calibration Preparation:**

1. **Warm-up Period:**
   - Minimum 30 minutes warm-up time
   - Stable operating temperature
   - Consistent power supply
   - No screen savers or power management

2. **Environment Setup:**
   - Ambient lighting: 5-10 nits (dim surround)
   - Wall color: Neutral gray (18% reflectance)
   - No direct light on screen
   - Controlled viewing environment

3. **Equipment:**
   - Colorimeter: Klein K10-A, X-Rite i1Display Pro, or JETI Specbos
   - Calibration software: Calman, LightSpace, or manufacturer software
   - Test pattern generator: Integrated or external
   - Verification patterns: Standard test patterns

**PQ (ST-2084) Calibration:**

**Step 1: Black Level Calibration**
- Display black test pattern (0%)
- Measure actual output luminance
- Adjust black level to target (typically 0.005 nits)
- Verify no light leakage

**Step 2: Peak Luminance Calibration**
- Display 100% white pattern
- Measure actual output luminance
- Adjust to target peak (e.g., 1000 nits)
- Verify stability over time

**Step 3: Grayscale Tracking**
- Measure grayscale from 0 to 100% in 10% steps
- Adjust RGB gains for neutral D65 (x=0.3127, y=0.3290)
- Verify ΔE < 2.0 across grayscale
- Document final RGB balance settings

**Step 4: EOTF Tracking**
- Measure actual EOTF curve vs ST-2084 target
- Generate lookup table for EOTF correction
- Verify tracking accuracy (ΔE < 2.0)
- Apply correction LUT to display

**Step 5: Color Gamut**
- Measure primary and secondary colors
- Verify Rec.2020 or P3 primary coordinates
- Adjust if necessary (some displays allow primary adjustment)
- Document actual gamut achieved

**Step 6: Verification**
- Display verification test patterns
- Measure critical points
- Verify overall calibration accuracy
- Document calibration results

**HLG Calibration:**

**Step 1: System Gamma Setup**
- Set system gamma target (1.2 for professional, 1.0 for reference)
- Configure OOTF processing
- Verify HLG signal path

**Step 2: Black Level**
- Same as PQ calibration
- Target 0.005 nits typical

**Step 3: Peak Luminance**
- Same as PQ calibration
- Target 1000 nits typical

**Step 4: Grayscale and Color**
- Same as PQ calibration
- D65 white point target
- Color gamut verification

**Step 5: System Gamma Verification**
- Measure actual OOTF vs target
- Verify system gamma applied correctly
- Test with HLG test patterns

### Consumer Display Setup

**Dolby Vision TVs:**

**Setup for QC (Quality Control):**
1. **Picture Mode Selection:**
   - Select "Cinema" or "Movie" mode
   - Disable "Vivid" or "Store" modes
   - Disable all enhancements (motion smoothing, sharpness, etc.)

2. **HDR Settings:**
   - Dolby Vision: Enable "Cinema" mode
   - HDR10: Use "Cinema" preset
   - Disable "Dynamic Contrast"
   - Set peak brightness to maximum (or specific target)

3. **Color Settings:**
   - Color Temperature: Warm or Normal (avoid Cool)
   - Color Gamut: Auto or Native
   - Disable color enhancements

4. **Advanced Settings:**
   - Disable motion interpolation (soap opera effect)
   - Disable edge enhancement
   - Disable noise reduction (or set to low)
   - Enable 24p mode for film content

**General HDR TV Setup:**

**Critical Settings to Disable:**
- Eco/Auto power saving (affects peak brightness)
- Motion smoothing/frame interpolation
- Dynamic contrast/artificial contrast
- Edge enhancement/sharpness
- Noise reduction (or minimal)
- Any "AI" or "Smart" picture modes

**Recommended Settings:**
- Picture Mode: Cinema, Movie, or Calibrated
- Backlight: Maximum (for HDR)
- Contrast: Default or calibrated value
- Brightness: Default (affects black level)
- Color: Default or calibrated value
- Tint: Default or calibrated value

### Validation and Quality Control

**Technical Validation:**

**Test Pattern Suite:**
1. **Grayscale Ramp:** Verify neutral tracking across range
2. **Color Bars:** Verify color accuracy and saturation
3. **EOTF Ramp:** Verify ST-2084 or HLG tracking
4. **Gamut Patterns:** Verify color primaries and secondaries
5. **Real-World Content:** Verify with known reference material

**Measurement Verification:**
- Spot check key measurements
- Verify black level and peak brightness
- Check grayscale neutrality
- Verify color accuracy
- Document results

**Visual Validation:**
- Display reference content
- Compare with calibrated reference
- Evaluate skin tone reproduction
- Verify HDR highlight appearance
- Check shadow detail visibility

**Cross-Display Validation:**
- Test on multiple consumer displays
- Test on both OLED and LCD
- Verify appearance across different peak brightness levels
- Document any inconsistencies
- Make creative adjustments if necessary

## Failure Modes and Troubleshooting

### Wrong Monitor Settings

**Symptoms:**
- Incorrect brightness or contrast
- Color cast or tint
- Poor HDR appearance
- Inconsistent results

**Common Issues:**

**Vivid or Store Mode:**
- Extremely bright and oversaturated
- Completely inaccurate color
- Not suitable for professional work
- Always avoid for critical evaluation

**Motion Smoothing:**
- "Soap opera effect" on film content
- Alters temporal appearance
- Can introduce artifacts
- Always disable for accurate evaluation

**Dynamic Contrast:**
- Artificially alters image contrast
- Inconsistent appearance over time
- Can crush shadows or clip highlights
- Always disable for accurate HDR evaluation

**Solutions:**
- Use Cinema, Movie, or Calibrated picture mode
- Disable all image enhancements
- Use professional calibration when possible
- Document settings for reproducibility
- Regularly verify settings remain correct

### ABL Confusion

**Symptoms:**
- Inconsistent brightness across different content
- Peak brightness varies unexpectedly
- HDR impact seems reduced
- Difficulty evaluating content

**Understanding ABL:**

**OLED ABL Behavior:**
- Small highlights: Full peak brightness available
- Large bright areas: Reduced peak brightness
- Content-dependent: Varies with scene characteristics
- Normal operation: Not a defect

**Example:**
- 2% window: 1000 nits peak
- 10% window: 700-800 nits peak
- 25% window: 400-500 nits peak
- 100% window: 200-300 nits peak

**Working with ABL:**
- Understand ABL behavior when evaluating content
- Test with various brightness distributions
- Don't expect full peak with full-screen white
- Consider ABL when making creative decisions
- Test on non-OLED displays for comparison

**Verification:**
- Test with small highlight patterns
- Test with large bright areas
- Observe ABL behavior
- Document findings
- Adjust evaluation criteria accordingly

### Peak Brightness Mismatch

**Symptoms:**
- Content looks too bright or too dark
- HDR impact inconsistent with expectations
- Different appearance on different displays
- Tone mapping issues

**Root Causes:**
- Mastering display peak doesn't match target consumer displays
- Grading for higher peak than typical consumer capabilities
- Not testing on variety of displays
- Not considering ABL behavior

**Best Practices:**
- Master on 1000-nit display for HDR10 (even if content can peak higher)
- Test on 600-nit consumer displays
- Test on 1000-4000-nit consumer displays
- Consider display technology differences
- Grade for typical consumer capabilities, not edge cases

**Testing Protocol:**
1. Master on professional reference display
2. Test on 600-nit consumer OLED
3. Test on 1000-nit consumer LCD
4. Test on 4000-nit high-end consumer display
5. Verify acceptable appearance across range
6. Document any issues or limitations

### Calibration Drift

**Symptoms:**
- Color accuracy degrades over time
- Brightness levels shift
- Inconsistent appearance
- Unexpected color casts

**Causes:**
- OLED panel aging (organic degradation)
- LCD backlight degradation
- Temperature variations
- Power supply variations
- Mechanical stress

**Prevention:**
- Regular calibration schedule (annually for professional monitors)
- Daily warm-up before critical work
- Stable operating environment
- Consistent usage patterns
- Monitor performance regularly

**Detection:**
- Visual inspection against reference
- Regular measurement verification
- Comparison with reference displays
- Monitoring calibration dates
- Documenting calibration history

**Solutions:**
- Schedule regular professional calibration
- Use displays with self-calibration features
- Maintain calibration records
- Plan for display replacement when appropriate
- Use calibration verification tools

## Mini Glossary

**Peak luminance** - Maximum brightness level display can produce, measured in nits, critical for HDR impact and content mastering.

**ABL** [Automatic Brightness Limiter] - Power management feature in OLED displays that reduces peak brightness when large areas of screen are bright, affecting HDR rendering.

**Nit** - Unit of luminance equal to one candela per square meter (cd/m²), standard measurement for display brightness.

**PQ monitoring** - Displaying PQ-encoded HDR content on ST-2084 calibrated monitor with appropriate peak luminance and EOTF tracking.

**HLG monitoring** - Displaying HLG-encoded HDR content on BT.2100 calibrated monitor with appropriate system gamma (1.2 professional, 1.0 reference).

**Calibration** - Process of adjusting display to meet specific technical criteria for color accuracy, EOTF tracking, and luminance levels, critical for professional HDR work.

**OLED** [Organic Light Emitting Diode] - Display technology with self-emissive pixels providing perfect black levels and excellent viewing angles, with ABL limitations for sustained peak brightness.

**LCD** [Liquid Crystal Display] - Display technology using backlight with local dimming zones for improved contrast, with higher peak brightness than OLED but not true black.

**Local dimming** - LCD technology dividing display into independently controlled zones for improved contrast, with blooming artifacts around high-contrast edges.

**Blooming** - LCD artifact where bright areas bleed into adjacent dark zones, creating halos around bright objects on dark backgrounds.

**Professional monitor** - HDR reference display meeting specific technical criteria for color accuracy, EOTF tracking, and calibration, typically 1000-nit peak minimum for HDR work.

**Consumer display** - Home HDR display with varying capabilities (600-4000 nits peak), used for quality control and validation of HDR content.

**Viewing environment** - Room conditions affecting perceived image quality, including ambient lighting, wall color, and surround luminance, critical for accurate HDR evaluation.

**Colorimeter** - Device for measuring display color and luminance, used for calibration and verification (Klein K10-A, X-Rite i1Display Pro, JETI Specbos).

**Warm-up period** - Time required for display to reach stable operation after powering on, typically 30 minutes for professional HDR monitors.

**Surround** - Ambient lighting and wall reflectance in viewing environment, ideally neutral gray at 5-10 nits for HDR evaluation.

**System gamma** - Adjustment applied to HLG signal for optimal contrast appearance, 1.2 for professional mastering, 1.0 for reference.

**EOTF tracking** - Accuracy of display's electro-optical transfer function compared to target curve (ST-2084 or BT.2100), critical for accurate HDR rendering.

**ΔE** [Delta E] - Color difference metric, ΔE < 2.0 considered imperceptible to most viewers, target for display calibration accuracy.

**Reference material** - Known-good HDR content used for validation and comparison during calibration and quality control processes.

**Drift** - Gradual change in display performance over time due to panel aging, component degradation, or environmental factors, requiring periodic recalibration.

**Self-calibration** - Display feature enabling automated calibration using built-in sensors, available on some professional monitors for maintaining accuracy.

**Validation** - Process of verifying display performance meets requirements through measurement and visual inspection, critical for quality assurance.

### Sony BVM-HX3110 — The Established 4000-Nit Reference

The **Sony BVM-HX3110** (30.5-inch 4K TRIMASTER HX) has become the established flagship reference mastering monitor for Hollywood grading suites as of 2026. It is the direct successor to the BVM-HX310 (which remains available) and represents Sony's current pinnacle of HDR reference technology.

**Key Specifications:**
- **Panel:** 30.5-inch 4K TRIMASTER HX dual-layer anti-reflection LCD
- **Peak Luminance:** 4000 cd/m² (4000 nits) — the highest sustained peak brightness of any professional reference monitor as of 2026
- **Color Gamut:** 100% Rec.2020, 100% DCI-P3
- **Connectivity:** 12G-SDI, HDMI, IP interface for SMPTE ST2110
- **Optional Licenses:** HDR-SDR conversion, 3D LUT, 4K-HD conversion
- **Technology:** Optical wave-canceling technology for ambient light reduction

**DIT/Colorist Implications:** The 4000-nit peak brightness is critical for HDR mastering at the highest tier (Dolby Vision 4000-nit theatrical, premium streaming). The BVM-HX3110 is the de facto standard for Netflix, Disney+, and Apple TV+ HDR mastering specifications. However, the BVM-HX310 (1000 nits) remains the workhorse for the majority of HDR10 and standard Dolby Vision (1000 nits) mastering. DITs should verify which reference monitor the post facility uses before assuming the mastering target.

### QD-OLED Reference Monitors — The New Affordable Reference Class

**ASUS ProArt PA27USD / PA32USD (NAB 2026):**
- **Panel:** 26.5-inch and 31.5-inch 4K QD-OLED (Quantum Dot OLED)
- **Peak Luminance:** ~1000 nits (sustained)
- **Price Point:** ~$2200 (significantly lower than Sony BVM series)
- **Key Features:** Auto-calibration built-in, 99% DCI-P3, 80% Rec.2020
- **Target Market:** Independent colorists, small post facilities, DIT carts, client review rooms

**Flanders Scientific XMP310:**
- **Panel:** QD-OLED reference monitor
- **Peak Luminance:** ~1000 nits
- **Target Market:** Professional grading suites as a secondary or client reference monitor

**EIZO Prototype (NAB 2026):**
- **Panel:** 1000-nit HDR OLED reference monitor
- **Status:** Still in prototype stage as of May 2026
- **Expected:** Production models targeted for late 2026 or 2027

**Significance:** QD-OLED reference monitors represent a major shift in the HDR reference monitor market. Traditional OLED reference monitors (Sony BVM, Canon DP-V) cost $15,000–$40,000, making them accessible only to major post facilities. The QD-OLED monitors at ~$2200 bring reference-grade HDR monitoring (1000 nits, wide gamut, self-emissive blacks) to independent colorists, DIT carts, and client review rooms. The trade-off is peak brightness (1000 nits vs. 4000 nits) and Rec.2020 coverage (~80% vs. 100%), but for the vast majority of HDR10 and standard Dolby Vision (1000 nits) work, the QD-OLED monitors are sufficient.

### Calibration Standards Update (2026)

**CalMAN Apple CMF 2026:**
- Portrait Displays released an updated CalMAN workflow in 2026 specifically optimized for Apple displays (Studio Display, Pro Display XDR, and MacBook Pro Liquid Retina XDR panels).
- Includes updated EOTF tracking targets for Apple's proprietary display processing, which differs from standard PQ/HLG curves.
- Not a replacement for professional reference monitors, but provides a standardized calibration path for Apple displays used in DIT carts and client review.

**ColourSpace ZRO FCVM:**
- ColourSpace introduced the "ZRO" (Zero Reference Offset) Full Chain Verification Method, which measures the entire color pipeline from source to display in a single pass, rather than calibrating display and source separately.
- Particularly relevant for VP workflows where the LED wall is both source and display simultaneously.
