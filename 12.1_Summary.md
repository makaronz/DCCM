# 12.1 Summary & Key Concepts for DITs and Colorists

A cheat sheet. The stuff you forget at 3 AM on set and need to look up in ten seconds.

## Core Principles

- **Color Consistency:** Keep the image looking the same from camera to delivery. What you see on set should match what the colorist sees in the suite, which should match what audiences see in theaters or on Netflix.
- **Scene-Referred vs. Display-Referred:** Scene-referred = the light that actually hit the sensor. Display-referred = what your monitor outputs. Don't confuse the two. Grade in scene-referred space, preview in display-referred space.
- **Color Spaces:** Rec. 709 for HD broadcast. DCI-P3 for cinema. Rec. 2020 for UHD/HDR. ACES if you want one pipeline that handles everything.
- **Gamma and Log:** Gamma encodes brightness efficiently. Log curves preserve dynamic range by mimicking film response. Neither is "better" — they serve different purposes.
- **LUTs:** 1D LUTs fix contrast/gamma. 3D LUTs fix hue shifts. Technical LUTs convert color spaces. Creative LUTs give you a look. Don't use a creative LUT when you need a technical one.
- **ACES:** Camera-independent pipeline. Convert everything to ACES at ingest, grade in ACEScc/ACEScct, output to whatever display your client needs. ACES 2.0 is the current version (released April 2025).
- **HDR:** HDR10 is the baseline. Dolby Vision adds dynamic metadata scene-by-scene. HDR10+ is Samsung's answer to Dolby Vision. HLG is for broadcast. PQ (ST-2084) is for mastered content. Know which one your delivery spec requires.
- **Calibration:** If your monitor isn't calibrated, you're grading blind. Period. Calibrate monthly with a spectroradiometer. Verify daily with a colorimeter.
- **AI and ML (Chapter 13):** AI color matching, denoising, and upscaling are now standard tools. Use them as starting points, not final decisions. Always verify skin tones manually. Disclose AI use in credits.
- **Sustainability (Chapter 14):** Post-production has a carbon footprint. Cloud rendering region matters (Iceland = near-zero carbon, India = high carbon). LTO-9 tape beats hard drives for archival. Turn off your DIT cart during lunch.

## Key Responsibilities of a DIT

- **On-Set Color Management:** LUT boxes, live grading, calibrated monitors. Make sure the DP sees what they're supposed to see.
- **Data Management:** Back up everything. Twice. Verify checksums. Losing footage is not an option.
- **Exposure Monitoring:** Waveform, false color, zebras. Don't let highlights clip. Don't crush shadows unless the DP asks for it.
- **On-Set Looks:** Build LUTs/CDLs with the DP that preview the final look. These travel with the footage to post.
- **Communication:** Tell post what you did. Document LUTs, color space, exposure decisions. Assume the colorist wasn't on set.
- **Quality Control:** Watch for banding, noise, artifacts, dead pixels. Catch it now, not in the DI suite.

## Key Responsibilities of a Colorist

- **Color Grading:** Make it look good. Make it look consistent. Make it look like the DP intended.
- **Color Space Management:** Know your working space (ACEScg, DaVinci Wide Gamut, camera native). Know your delivery space. Convert correctly.
- **Technical Expertise:** Understand codecs, bit depth, HDR metadata, and why a ProRes 422 proxy looks different from the RAW master.
- **Creative Collaboration:** The DP and director are your partners, not your bosses. Push back when a request breaks the image.
- **Mastering and Delivery:** One master rarely covers all platforms. Netflix wants Dolby Vision. Amazon accepts HDR10+. Apple requires DV + Atmos. Know the spec matrix.

## Workflow Summary

1. **Pre-Production:**
   - Pick cameras, formats, color spaces.
   - Choose ACES or camera-native pipeline.
   - Calibrate monitors. Test the workflow end-to-end.

2. **Production (On-Set):**
   - Set camera white balance, ISO, format.
   - Monitor exposure with waveform/false color.
   - Build LUTs with the DP.
   - Back up footage. Verify checksums.
   - Talk to post. Document everything.

3. **Post-Production (Editorial):**
   - Import with LUTs/CDLs applied.
   - Edit with proxies. Don't touch the RAW.

4. **Post-Production (Color):**
   - Conform from RAW/Log originals.
   - Apply input transforms.
   - Grade in wide-gamut working space.
   - Output to delivery specs (SDR, HDR10, DV, etc.).

5. **Archival:**
   - Multiple copies. Multiple locations.
   - LTO-9 for long-term. Cloud cold storage for accessibility.
   - Verify integrity every 2–3 years.

## When Things Go Wrong

- **Color drift between set and post:** Check that the on-set LUT uses the same color space as the post pipeline. Document the transform chain.
- **HDR looks wrong on client's TV:** Client TVs are uncalibrated. Provide a reference chart at the start of every review. Don't let them judge color on a $200 Best Buy special.
- **AI denoiser destroyed skin texture:** AI denoising is a blunt instrument. Use spatial NR for chroma, temporal NR for luma. Check pores at 200% zoom.
- **Cloud render cost 3x budget:** Cloud GPU instances charge by the hour. An A100 costs $12.50/hour. A 40-hour grading week is $500. Factor this in before you pitch cloud grading to the producer.
- **LED wall color doesn't match the real location:** Calibrate with OpenVPCal before shooting. Re-calibrate weekly. Verify the renderer LUT is loaded. Check loop seams on pre-rendered content.

## The One-Sentence Rules

- Calibrate your monitor or don't call yourself a colorist.
- Document every LUT. Future-you will thank present-you.
- RAW gives you options. Log gives you efficiency. H.264 gives you neither.
- HDR is not "SDR but brighter." It's a different image that needs different decisions.
- AI is a tool, not a replacement. The colorist's eye is still the final arbiter.
- Sustainability is not a marketing checkbox. It's a cost-saving measure (LTO tape is cheaper than cloud storage long-term).
- When in doubt, measure. When still in doubt, measure again.
