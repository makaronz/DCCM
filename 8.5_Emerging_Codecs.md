# 8.5 Emerging Codecs

## Theory: Next-Generation Compression Standards

### AV1 (AOMedia Video 1)

**AV1** [Alliance for Open Media Video 1] represents a paradigm shift in royalty-free video compression, designed from the ground up to compete with HEVC/H.265 while remaining completely free of licensing fees. Developed by the Alliance for Open Media (AOM) — founding members include Amazon, Apple, Facebook, Google, Intel, Microsoft, Netflix, and NVIDIA — AV1 achieves approximately **30% better compression than HEVC** at the same visual quality level, with computational complexity increasing by roughly 3-5x for encoding and 1.5-2x for decoding.

The codec employs several advanced encoding tools:

- **Partitioning hierarchy**: Superblocks (128×128 or 64×64) recursively split down to 4×4 using quadtree, binary, or ternary tree structures, providing far more flexible motion representation than HEVC's fixed quadtree
- **Prediction modes**: 65 angular intra prediction modes (HEVC has 35), plus palette mode for screen content and filter-intra mode that applies directional filters before prediction
- **Transform types**: Multiple transform sizes (4×4 to 64×64) with DCT, ADST (Asymmetric Discrete Sine Transform), and flip-adaptive transforms, allowing better energy compaction for different residual patterns
- **Loop restoration**: Switchable restoration filters (Wiener filter or self-guided filter) applied after deblocking for enhanced quality, particularly effective at low bitrates
- **Film grain synthesis**: Synthesizes film grain at decode time rather than encoding it, preserving intentional film texture without wasting bits on noise that would be difficult to compress efficiently

AV1 supports **4:2:0, 4:2:2, and 4:4:4 chroma sampling** with bit depths up to 12-bit. High Dynamic Range is supported through the **Ambient Viewing Environment metadata** (AV1 refers to it as "metadata for high dynamic range and wide color gamut"), which can carry HDR10, HDR10+, and Dolby Vision-compatible metadata. The codec also supports **Film grain synthesis** parameters, enabling intentional grain reproduction from analog sources.

### JPEG XS (SMPTE ST-2117)

**JPEG XS** [ISO/IEC 21177 | SMPTE ST-2117] occupies a unique position as a **mezzanine codec** designed for visually lossless compression with ultra-low latency (sub-1ms to sub-10ms). Unlike traditional JPEG (DCT-based, high compression, high quality loss) or JPEG 2000 (wavelet-based, scalable quality), JPEG XS targets **"visually lossless"** compression ratios of 6:1 to 10:1 while maintaining **sub-millisecond latency** — critical for live production, contribution feeds, and real-time processing where codec delay disrupts synchronization.

Standardized as **SMPTE ST-2117-1** (Video-over-IP compression for contribution) and **SMPTE ST-2117-2** (Audio-over-IP compression), JPEG XS operates through these mechanisms:

- **DCT-based transform**: Uses 4×4 and 8×8 discrete cosine transforms with integer arithmetic, avoiding wavelet transforms (JPEG 2000) that require more computational resources
- **Quantization**: Visually optimized quantization matrices based on human visual system contrast sensitivity, allocating fewer bits to high-frequency components that are less perceptible
- **Bitstream slicing**: Supports slicing (independent regions within a frame) for parallel processing and error resilience, enabling hardware acceleration and partial frame decoding
- **Color format support**: Native support for **RGB 4:4:4**, YCbCr 4:4:4, 4:2:2, and 4:2:0 with bit depths up to 16-bit, making it suitable for high-end production workflows that preserve color fidelity
- **Quality control**: Adjustable quality parameter (1-15, where 15 is visually lossless) and bitrate control modes (constant quality, constant bitrate, or constrained variable bitrate)

JPEG XS is specifically engineered for **IP-based production environments** where bandwidth conservation is critical but quality must be maintained. Typical applications include:
- **Contribution feeds** transporting 4K/UHD signals from remote venues to broadcast centers (e.g., sports events, live concerts)
- **Studio router infrastructure** replacing SDI cables with 10GbE/25GbE networks
- **Virtual production** where LED wall content must be processed with minimal delay to match camera capture
- **Remote monitoring** sending high-quality monitoring feeds to off-site directors and colorists

### VVC/H.266 (Versatile Video Coding)

**VVC** [Versatile Video Coding | ITU-T H.266 | ISO/IEC 23090-3], standardized in 2020, achieves **approximately 50% bitrate reduction compared to HEVC** at the same subjective quality, representing the most substantial compression gain in the generational timeline (H.264 → H.265 → H.266). However, this performance comes with **exponential increases** in computational complexity: encoding complexity is roughly **10-30x higher than HEVC**, with decoding complexity 2-3x higher. As of 2025, hardware decoding support remains limited, restricting VVC primarily to research and specialized applications.

Key technical innovations include:

- **Quadtree with nested multi-type tree (QT-MTT) partitioning**: Beyond HEVC's quadtree + binary/ternary splits, VVC adds more flexible partitioning modes, allowing blocks to be divided into rectangular shapes with horizontal or vertical splits of varying ratios
- **Affine motion compensation**: Models rotation, zoom, and skew motion (rather than just translational), significantly improving prediction for rotating objects, camera pans, and warping effects that challenge block-based motion models
- **63 intra prediction modes**: Expanded from HEVC's 35 modes, including matrix-based intra prediction (MIP) that learns prediction filters through offline training
- **Adaptive loop filter (ALF)**: Applies filters whose coefficients are calculated based on local statistics, effectively learning optimal filtering for each block rather than using fixed filters
- **Transform selection**: Multiple transform types including DCT-8, DST-7, and a transform-skip mode, selected adaptively based on residual characteristics

VVC supports **4:2:0, 4:2:2, and 4:4:4 chroma** with bit depths up to 12-bit and resolutions up to **16K** (16,384 × 8,448 pixels). HDR support includes **SL-HDR (Philips)**, **HDR10+**, and **technical metadata** for PQ/HLG transfer functions. The standard also introduces **Screen Content Coding (SCC)** tools for graphics and text, including intra block copy, palette mode, and adaptive color transforms.

### EVC (MPEG-5 Part 1: Essential Video Coding)

**EVC** [Essential Video Coding | ISO/IEC 23094-1], standardized in 2020, takes a different approach by dividing the codec into a **baseline profile** (royalty-free) and **enhanced tools** (patent-encumbered, requiring licensing). This modular design allows adoption of the baseline without fear of future patent assertions, while enhanced tools provide performance improvements through patented technologies that must be licensed separately.

**Baseline profile** (17 tools, royalty-free) achieves approximately **30% better compression than H.264/AVC** with reasonable computational complexity comparable to HEVC Main profile. It includes:
- **Affine prediction** (limited to 4-parameter affine vs VVC's 6-parameter)
- **Adaptive loop filter** (simplified version of VVC's ALF)
- **Large block partitioning** (up to 128×128)
- **Transform skip mode** for lossless regions

**Enhanced profiles** add 7 additional tools (including:
- **Enhanced intra prediction** (65 modes)
- **Cross-component prediction** (predicting chroma from luma)
- **Enhanced quantization** (adaptive rounding, quantization matrices)

With enhanced tools enabled, EVC approaches HEVC performance (~90-95% of HEVC quality at same bitrate) while providing a royalty-free baseline for applications sensitive to licensing costs (e.g., broadcast contribution, surveillance, medical imaging).

**As of 2025**, EVC adoption remains limited compared to AV1 and VVC, with primary interest in cost-sensitive applications where HEVC licensing is prohibitive but AV1 complexity is excessive.

## Practice: Emerging Codec Implementation

### AV1 for Streaming and Distribution

**Adoption timeline (2023-2025)**: AV1 decoder support has reached critical mass in consumer devices:
- **Hardware decoding**: All major GPU manufacturers (NVIDIA RTX 40-series, AMD RX 7000-series, Intel Arc) include AV1 hardware decode; mobile SoCs (Qualcomm Snapdragon 8 Gen 2+, Apple A17 Pro, Google Tensor G3) provide hardware acceleration
- **Browser support**: Chrome 70+, Firefox 67+, Edge 79+, Safari 17+ (macOS 14+)
- **Streaming platforms**: Netflix (2020), YouTube (2021), Disney+ (2022), HBO Max (2023) encode AV1 for bandwidth-constrained viewers

**Encoding workflows** for AV1 in production environments:

1. **Software encoding**: **aomenc** (reference encoder, very slow), **libaom-av1** (moderately optimized), **SVT-AV1** (Intel/AMD Scalable Video Technology, 3-10x faster than libaom, production-ready), **rav1e** (rust-based, 2-3x faster than libaom, quality-focused)

2. **Hardware acceleration**: **NVIDIA NVENC** (RTX 40-series with AV1 encoding, 3-5x real-time for 1080p, 1-2x for 4K), **Intel Quick Sync** (Arc GPUs, 2-3x real-time for 1080p), **AMD VCE** (RX 7000-series, limited availability in 2024)

3. **Codec parameters for quality**:
   - **CPU usage preset** (`--cpu-used` in aomenc/SVT-AV1): 0 (slowest, best quality) to 8 (fastest). Recommended: 4-5 for offline encoding, 6-7 for live streaming
   - **Tile columns/rows** (`--tile-columns`, `--tile-rows`): Enable parallel decoding. For 4K: 4 tile columns, 2 tile rows (16 tiles) recommended for multi-core decoding
   - **Bit depth**: 10-bit required for HDR (`--bit-depth=10`)
   - **Chroma subsampling**: 4:2:0 for streaming, 4:2:2/4:4:4 for mezzanine/archival
   - **Rate control**: Constant rate factor (CRF) recommended for VOD (`--end-usage=q`, `--cq-level=28-32` for high quality)

**Quality comparison**: At 4K HDR, AV1 achieves visually transparent quality at **~12-15 Mbps** for typical film content, compared to HEVC at **18-22 Mbps** and H.264 at **35-45 Mbps**. For HD SDR, AV1 reaches transparency at **4-6 Mbps**, versus HEVC at **7-9 Mbps** and H.264 at **12-15 Mbps**.

**Production considerations**:
- **Encoding time**: SVT-AV1 on 32-core CPU encodes 4K HDR at approximately **0.8-1.5x real-time** (slow for live, acceptable for VOD). Hardware encoders provide **2-5x real-time** but with 10-15% quality penalty
- **Delivery format**: MP4 container with `av01.0.12M.10` codec string (profile 0, level 12, bit depth 10), Matroska/WebM also widely supported
- **Compatibility check**: Test playback on target devices (smart TVs older than 2022 may lack AV1 decode); provide HEVC/H.264 fallback for legacy devices

### JPEG XS for Contribution and Production

**Hardware availability (2024-2025)**: JPEG XS encoders/decoders are available from:
- **intoPIX** (TicoXS SDK, FPGA implementations, licenses to manufacturers)
- **Barco** (encoders/decoders for contribution feeds)
- **AWS Elemental** (live processing supports JPEG XS)
- **Haivision** (contribution codecs)

**Typical applications** in live production:

1. **Remote contribution** (sports/event coverage): Transport 4K 60fps HDR signals over 10GbE networks at **~3-4 Gbps** (visually lossless, 6:1 compression from uncompressed 12 Gbps). Compared to JPEG 2000, JPEG XS reduces latency from **100-200ms** to **<10ms**, eliminating lip-sync issues.

2. **Studio router replacement**: Replace SDI router infrastructure with IP-based JPEG XS streams over 25GbE, supporting hundreds of concurrent HD/4K signals with visually lossless quality and minimal latency.

3. **Virtual production**: LED wall content generated from Unreal Engine must synchronize with camera capture at frame-accurate precision. JPEG XS's sub-1ms latency ensures alignment without introducing timing offsets that would cause perspective mismatches between real and virtual elements.

**Quality settings** for JPEG XS:
- **Quality parameter**: 12-15 for visually lossless (quality 15 = near-lossless, compression ratio 4:1-6:1; quality 12 = visually lossless, compression ratio 8:1-10:1)
- **Bit depth**: 10-bit or 12-bit YCbCr 4:2:2 or RGB 4:4:4
- **Profile**: Main Profile (4:2:0/4:2:2/4:4:4), High Profile (adds 4:4:4 and 16-bit support)
- **Level**: Based on resolution and frame rate (Level 12K supports up to 7680×4320 at 60fps)

**Workflow integration**:
- **Container**: Raw RTP streams (ST 2117-20), MXF (ST 2117-30), or MPEG-TS (legacy compatibility)
- **Network**: Requires **10GbE minimum for 4K**, 25GbE/40GbE for multi-stream environments. PTP (Precision Time Protocol) synchronization for genlock alignment
- **Monitoring**: JPEG XS decoders must output SDI or NDI for monitoring, with latency measured end-to-end

**Practical guidance**:
- **Adopt AV1** for new streaming workflows (2024+), especially for bandwidth-constrained delivery (mobile, international). SVT-AV1 provides acceptable encoding speed for VOD; hardware encoders for live.
- **Consider JPEG XS** for contribution and studio infrastructure when replacing SDI with IP (2025+). Requires investment in encoders/decoders but provides substantial bandwidth savings with minimal quality penalty.
- **Wait on VVC** until hardware decode support becomes widespread (expected 2026-2027). Currently viable only for offline archival encoding where decode complexity is not a constraint.
- **EVC remains niche**: Consider only if HEVC licensing is prohibitive and AV1 complexity is excessive (e.g., embedded systems, medical imaging, surveillance).

## Failure Modes: Emerging Codec Issues

### Codec Compatibility

**Problem**: AV1, VVC, and JPEG XS require decoder support in playback devices. Encountering compatibility issues leads to failed playback, pixelated video, or audio-video sync drift.

**Causes**:
- **Decoder absence**: Older smart TVs, streaming sticks, and mobile devices lack hardware decoder support for AV1 (pre-2022 devices) or any support for VVC/JPEG XS
- **Software decode limitations**: Software decoding AV1/VVC on underpowered CPUs causes stuttering, frame drops, or audio desync (e.g., 4K AV1 on dual-core laptop)
- **Profile/level mismatches**: Encoding with features unsupported by target decoders (e.g., AV1 12-bit 4:4:4 for streaming when most devices only support 10-bit 4:2:0)

**Detection**:
- **Playback testing**: Verify encoded files play on target devices (actual hardware, not just software decoders)
- **Codec string analysis**: Check media info (ffprobe, MediaInfo) for profile/level/bit depth/chroma subsampling
- **Decode capability query**: Some players report supported codecs (e.g., `chrome://gpu` in Chrome shows AV1 hardware decode support)

**Prevention**:
- **Multi-codec delivery**: Encode adaptive bitrate ladder with both AV1 and HEVC (or H.264) for widest compatibility. Use manifest formats (HLS, DASH) to select appropriate codec per device
- **Profile constraints**: For streaming, use AV1 Main profile (10-bit 4:2:0) rather than High profile (12-bit 4:2:2/4:4:4)
- **Quality tiers**: Offer AV1 for premium/bandwidth-rich viewers, HEVC/H.264 fallback for others

**Recovery**:
- **Re-encode**: Re-export file with compatible settings (lower profile, different codec)
- **Transcode on-the-fly**: Use media server (e.g., AWS Elemental, Wowza) to transcode to compatible format for unsupported devices

### Licensing Issues

**Problem**: HEVC (H.265), VVC (H.266), and MPEG-5 EVC enhanced tools require licensing from patent pools (HEVC Advance, Velos Media, MPEG LA). Failure to obtain licenses exposes organizations to legal liability, especially for commercial distribution.

**Causes**:
- **Lack of awareness**: Assuming HEVC is free (common misconception; H.264 is largely royalty-free for free internet video, but HEVC is not)
- **Unclear licensing terms**: HEVC licensing involves multiple patent pools with overlapping claims, creating complexity in determining total fees
- **Open-source misinterpretation**: Using open-source encoders (x265, libaom) does not exempt users from patent licensing obligations

**Detection**:
- **Legal review**: Consult intellectual property counsel before deploying HEVC/VVC for commercial distribution
- **Codec selection**: AV1, JPEG XS (under certain conditions), and EVC baseline profile are royalty-free alternatives

**Prevention**:
- **Codec choice**: For commercial distribution without licensing resources, choose AV1 (royalty-free) or H.264 (largely royalty-free for free internet video)
- **Licensing agreements**: Obtain licenses from HEVC Advance, Velos Media, and MPEG LA for HEVC/VVC as needed
- **EVC baseline**: Use EVC baseline profile for royalty-free compression if AV1 complexity is prohibitive

**Recovery**:
- **Cease distribution**: Stop distributing unlicensed content
- **Retroactive licensing**: Negotiate licenses for past distribution (may involve penalties)
- **Re-encode**: Transition to royalty-free codecs (AV1, EVC baseline) for future content

### Playback Support

**Problem**: Encoded video fails to play correctly on target platforms due to missing codecs, unsupported container formats, or incompatible parameters.

**Causes**:
- **Missing codec decoder**: Device or software lacks decoder for AV1, VVC, or JPEG XS
- **Container incompatibility**: AV1 in MP4 requires specific brand (`av01` codec string in `stsd` box); older players may not recognize it
- **HDR metadata issues**: AV1 HDR metadata (Ambient Viewing Environment) not correctly mapped to display, causing incorrect tone mapping
- **High bitrate playback**: Network or storage insufficient for JPEG XS contribution streams (3-4 Gbps for 4K)

**Detection**:
- **Platform testing**: Test on actual target platforms (iOS, Android, smart TV brands, web browsers)
- **Network bandwidth monitoring**: Verify network can sustain bitrate (especially for JPEG XS contribution)
- **Codec capability databases**: Consult platform documentation (e.g., Netflix's partner guidelines, Apple's HLS specification)

**Prevention**:
- **Adaptive bitrate**: Use HLS/DASH with multiple codec variants (AV1, HEVC, H.264) to ensure at least one works per device
- **Container compliance**: Use MP4 with `av01` codec brand for AV1, or Matroska/WebM for broader support
- **Bitrate ladder**: Encode multiple quality tiers (1080p, 720p, 480p) to accommodate bandwidth constraints
- **Fallback mechanism**: Manifest should include H.264 baseline as ultimate fallback

**Recovery**:
- **Transcoding**: Re-encode for unsupported platforms (use cloud transcoding services like AWS Elemental, Cloudinary, Mux)
- **Delivery adjustment**: Lower bitrate or resolution for bandwidth-constrained viewers
- **Platform restriction**: Limit availability to supported devices (e.g., "4K HDR only on 2022+ devices")

## Mini Glossary

**AV1** [AOMedia Video 1] — Royalty-free video codec developed by the Alliance for Open Media (Amazon, Apple, Google, Netflix, etc.) achieving ~30% better compression than HEVC with 3-5x encoding complexity and 1.5-2x decoding complexity. Supports up to 12-bit 4:4:4, HDR (HDR10, HDR10+), and film grain synthesis. Adopted by major streaming platforms (Netflix, YouTube, Disney+) for bandwidth-efficient delivery. Reference encoder: `aomenc`, production encoders: SVT-AV1 (fast), rav1e (quality-focused). Hardware decode: RTX 40-series, RX 7000-series, Apple A17 Pro, Snapdragon 8 Gen 2+.

**JPEG XS** [ISO/IEC 21177 | SMPTE ST-2117] — Visually lossless mezzanine codec designed for ultra-low latency (sub-1ms to sub-10ms) contribution and production workflows. Achieves 6:1 to 10:1 compression with visually transparent quality, suitable for transporting 4K HDR over 10GbE at 3-4 Gbps. Uses DCT-based transform with visually optimized quantization, supporting RGB/YCbCr 4:4:4/4:2:2/4:2:0 up to 16-bit. Standardized for contribution (ST 2117-1), audio-over-IP (ST 2117-2), and essence carriage (ST 2117-20/30/40). Hardware available from intoPIX (TicoXS), Barco, AWS Elemental. Applications: contribution feeds, virtual production, studio routing.

**VVC** [Versatile Video Coding | ITU-T H.266 | ISO/IEC 23090-3] — Next-generation video codec standardized in 2020, achieving ~50% bitrate reduction over HEVC at same quality with 10-30x encoding complexity and 2-3x decoding complexity. Uses quadtree with nested multi-type tree (QT-MTT) partitioning, affine motion compensation, 63 intra modes, adaptive loop filter, and multiple transform types. Supports 4:2:0/4:2:2/4:4:4 up to 12-bit, resolutions up to 16K, and SL-HDR/HDR10+ metadata. As of 2025, hardware decode support remains limited, restricting adoption to research/archival. Expected hardware adoption: 2026-2027.

**EVC** [Essential Video Coding | MPEG-5 Part 1 | ISO/IEC 23094-1] — Modular codec with royalty-free baseline profile (17 tools, ~30% better than H.264) and enhanced tools (7 additional tools, ~90-95% of HEVC performance, licensing required). Designed to provide HEVC-level quality with a royalty-free option for cost-sensitive applications (broadcast contribution, surveillance, medical imaging). Baseline includes affine prediction, adaptive loop filter, large block partitioning (128×128), transform skip. Enhanced tools add 65 intra modes, cross-component prediction, enhanced quantization. Limited commercial adoption as of 2025.

**Contribution codec** — Codec used for transporting high-quality video from acquisition/production venues to broadcast centers or post-production facilities, prioritizing visual quality and low latency over compression efficiency. Examples: JPEG XS (ST 2117), JPEG 2000 (ST 2042), ProRes/ DNxHR (mezzanine), uncompressed (ST 2118). Contribution codecs typically operate at 3-10 Gbps for 4K, with latency requirements under 100ms (often <10ms for real-time productions). Distinct from distribution codecs (AV1, HEVC, H.264) that prioritize bitrate efficiency over quality/latency.

**Distribution codec** — Codec used for delivering video to end consumers (streaming, broadcast, physical media), prioritizing compression efficiency and widespread decoder support over maximum quality. Examples: AV1 (streaming, royalty-free), HEVC/H.265 (UHD Blu-ray, streaming, licensing fees), H.264/AVC (legacy compatibility), VVC (future). Distribution codecs typically operate at 4-25 Mbps for 4K, with higher latency acceptable for VOD (hours encode time) and lower latency for live (2-5 seconds). Must balance quality, bitrate, and compatibility across consumer devices.

**Visually lossless** — Compression quality level where differences between compressed and uncompressed video are imperceptible to human observers under normal viewing conditions. JPEG XS targets visually lossless quality at 6:1-10:1 compression using perceptually optimized quantization based on human visual system contrast sensitivity. Verified through psychophysical testing (double-blind A/B comparisons, DSIS methodology). Distinct from "near-lossless" (ProRes 4444 XQ, DNxHR 444) which has measurable but extremely low quality loss, and "transparent quality" (AV1/HEVC at 12-15 Mbps for 4K) where compression artifacts may be detectable in challenging scenes but generally imperceptible.

**AOM** [Alliance for Open Media] — Industry consortium developing royalty-free media codecs and formats. Founding members (2015): Amazon, ARM, Cisco, Google, Intel, Microsoft, Mozilla, Netflix, NVIDIA. Additional members: Apple, Facebook (Meta), Adobe, Samsung, Tencent, etc. Developed AV1 (2018), AV2 (in development), AV1 Image File Format (AVIF), and associated technologies (AV1 codec library, libaom). AV1 licensed under **BSD-2-Clause** (royalty-free, permissive). Contrast with HEVC/VVC licensing through patent pools (HEVC Advance, Velos Media, MPEG LA) requiring per-unit fees.

**ST-2117** [SMPTE ST-2117] — SMPTE standard suite for **visually lossless, low-latency video compression over IP** for contribution and production applications. ST 2117-1: Video-over-IP compression (JPEG XS), ST 2117-2: Audio-over-IP compression (MPEG-H 3D Audio, AAC), ST 2117-20: RTP carriage, ST 2117-30: MXF carriage, ST 2117-40: MPEG-TS carriage. Part of ST 2110 suite (IP-based real-time media). Enables transport of 4K 60fps HDR over 10GbE at 3-4 Gbps with <10ms latency, replacing SDI infrastructure with IP networks. Adopted by broadcast industry for remote production, virtual production, and studio routing.

**Encoding complexity** — Computational resources required to encode video, typically measured as **CPU/GPU time relative to real-time** (e.g., "3x real-time" means 1 hour of video takes 3 hours to encode). AV1: 3-5x real-time (SVT-AV1 on 32-core CPU), VVC: 10-30x real-time (reference encoder), HEVC: 1-3x real-time (x265 veryfast to medium), H.264: 0.5-2x real-time (x264 ultrafast to medium). Complexity increases with codec generation (H.264 → HEVC → VVC) and quality preset (faster = lower quality, slower = higher quality). Hardware encoders (NVENC, Quick Sync) provide 2-10x speedup with quality penalty.

**Decoding complexity** — Computational resources required to decode video, critical for playback devices with limited processing power (mobile, smart TVs). AV1: 1.5-2x H.264 decode complexity (software decode), VVC: 2-3x HEVC decode complexity. Hardware decode essential for 4K+ playback: RTX 40-series (AV1/VVC decode), Apple A17 Pro (AV1 hardware decode), Snapdragon 8 Gen 2+ (AV1 hardware decode). Software decode of 4K AV1 on underpowered CPUs causes frame drops/stuttering. Codec adoption constrained by decode hardware availability (VVC limited in 2025, expected mainstream 2026-2027).

### AV1 — Patent Risk Emerging

While AV1 is now the dominant next-gen OTT codec (Netflix ~30% of streams, YouTube ~75% of high-res traffic), **patent litigation risk has materialized**:

- **Dolby** filed patent infringement suits against Snap Inc. in **March 2026** targeting AV1 use.
- **Sisvel, InterDigital, and Nokia** are asserting **1,500+ patents** against AV1.
- **Netflix and Google** have **not paid Sisvel** as of 2026, creating uncertainty for the AV1 royalty-free narrative.
- The **Video Distribution Patent (VDP) Pool** (launched December 2025 by Access Advance) now covers streaming royalties for HEVC, VVC, VP9, and AV1.

**DIT/Post-Production Recommendation:**
- The **three-codec ladder** (AV1 primary, HEVC middle, H.264 fallback) remains the safest strategy for 2026 deliveries.
- Do not commit to AV1-only delivery unless the platform explicitly requires it and indemnifies the production against patent claims.
- For archival and long-term preservation, use **open intermediate formats** (ProRes 4444 XQ, DNxHR 444, OpenEXR) rather than AV1, to avoid future patent disputes affecting the master archive.

### MPEG-5 LCEVC — In Production Deployment

**MPEG-5 LCEVC (Low Complexity Enhancement Video Coding)** is now in production deployment as an **enhancement layer** atop any base codec (H.264, HEVC, AV1, VVC):

- **Brazil TV 3.0 (DTV+)** mandates **VVC + LCEVC** as the combined broadcast codec stack.
- LCEVC achieves **30–55% bitrate reduction** on top of the base codec, at very low computational cost.
- The enhancement layer can be implemented in **software on existing devices** without hardware decode support, making it backward-compatible.
- LCEVC is NOT a standalone codec — it is always an enhancement layer atop a base codec.

**DIT/Post-Production Implications:**
- LCEVC is relevant for **final broadcast encoding** only. DITs and post facilities do not work in LCEVC natively.
- If delivering to a Brazilian broadcaster or a platform adopting LCEVC, the encoding house must have an LCEVC-capable encoder (e.g., V-Nova, MainConcept).

### AV2 — Released May 2026

**AV2** was released by AOMedia in **May 2026**, targeting ~30% bitrate reduction vs. AV1. Hardware decode is expected **2027–2028**.

- AV2 is a **research and planning horizon** item for delivery pipelines. No production deployments as of May 2026.
- DITs and post supervisors should monitor AV2 hardware availability for projects with 2028+ release windows.
- Do not plan AV2-only delivery for any project releasing before 2029.

### SMPTE VC-6 and JPEG XS Market Status

- **SMPTE VC-6 (ST-2117):** Published as a mezzanine codec optimized for production workflows. Mapping into IMF (ST 2067-71) and MXF is underway. VC-6 is a **production codec candidate** for DITs and post facilities to monitor.
- **JPEG XS / ST 2110-22:** Now the dominant IP mezzanine format for broadcast and live production. $2.8 billion market in 2025, 250+ productions, 50+ manufacturers. Replacing uncompressed SDI in broadcast and VP workflows. For DITs working in broadcast or live VP, JPEG XS is the de facto standard for IP video transport.
- **SMPTE VC-5 (CineForm-derived):** Suite is complete; an IMF Application is in development. CineForm (now VC-5) may see a revival as an open intermediate codec for low-budget productions.

### ProRes RAW — Cross-Platform Breakthrough

DaVinci Resolve 20.2 (September 2025) added **native ProRes RAW decode on macOS, Windows, and Linux** — ending the Apple-only era:

- iPhone 17 Pro/Max can record ProRes RAW via external USB-C storage.
- Blackmagic Pocket Cinema Camera 4K and Video Assist 12G are scheduled to add ProRes RAW recording via firmware update.
- **DIT Implication:** ProRes RAW is now a viable cross-platform RAW format for productions mixing Apple and non-Apple workflows. Previously, ProRes RAW on Windows/Linux required third-party plugins or transcode to BRAW/REDCODE, adding complexity and potential color science mismatches.
